## Findings
- F-1 · Major · Goals, item 3 · "Nobody who can push a branch can reach the marketplace token or the npm publish" is an absolute claim the design does not meet: Risks (line 261) says a repository admin can change the environment's settings and the rulesets and "is outside this design", and admins can also push branches. A reader takes Goal 3 as covering admins and will not see that exclusion in the goals. The goal needs the same scope the Risks section states.
- F-2 · Major · Goals, item 6 · The goal says a release run names a token "within 30 days of its expiry". `token.ts` (line 172) passes with an "expiry unknown" warning when the expiry header is missing, and says the header's presence "is not relied on". So the 30-day warning is not guaranteed. A reader takes the goal as a guaranteed expiry check and misses that the email and the noted date are the real notice.
- F-3 · Major · Cost and duration · The heading promises a duration, and the Heilmeier standard asks how long the work will take, but the section gives only six phases and the author's setup steps. It has no time estimate and does not say why none is given. A reader cannot schedule the work from it.
- F-4 · Major · The writers of `<data dir>/bin`, `scripts/install.sh` · "`--force` anywhere among the arguments after `--download-only`" has two readings: only arguments that come after the `--download-only` flag, or any argument in a `--download-only` run. Two people would write two different parsers: `install.sh --force --download-only` either replaces or does not.
- F-5 · Nit · Goals, item 5 · "replaced only by a newer or equal version by any writer" is unqualified. A Non-Goal (line 49) and a Risk (line 268) exclude the shims already published (0.4.4). Also, "one stated command to go back" does not match the design, which gives two: `install --force` and `install.sh --force`.
- F-6 · Nit · Problem, line 29 · The example "an npm shim at 0.7.0" describes a shim that does not exist: line 15 says npm stopped at 0.4.4, and line 268 says machines hold 0.4.4. Use a version that was published, or say the example is hypothetical.
- F-7 · Nit · Problem, line 29 · "across a store schema bump the older binary then refuses" is hard to parse. Say that the older binary then refuses a store written by the newer schema (`SchemaTooNewError`).
- F-8 · Nit · Problem, line 29 · The closing line '"Newer" is not one rule.' is an aphoristic closer. The sentence before it already states the fact.
- F-9 · Nit · Design, "The door, and what it rests on" · "The door" is a coined metaphor that the reader has to decode. It is defined once and used only in this section, and "the environment `release` and its rule" says the same thing plainly. Also, the fourth of the "Four facts make it a door" is a hazard (GitHub creates an environment with no rule) plus the check that answers it, not a fact that makes the environment a barrier.
- F-10 · Nit · Design, "`plan`", the hold paragraph (line 130) · Two sentences are hard to read: "The constants unreadable at the tag, or the file present but unreadable at the previous one, holds `latest`" (subject and verb do not agree, and the subject is a noun phrase used as a condition) and "With `release_hold: true` a held `latest` is not held". Write both as plain conditions ("when … , `latest` is held"; "`release_hold: true` releases a hold").
- F-11 · Nit · Design, "Two workflows", line 78 · One sentence carries three changes, plus a parenthetical list of six job properties inside the third. Split it into one item per change.
- F-12 · Nit · Alternatives, "A hand copy of `older()` in each writer" · The reason given (the shell function in two files, the JavaScript function in two files, TypeScript in two callers) is also true of the chosen design (lines 182-184). The real difference, one shared table and the byte-for-byte tests, is only in the last four words.
- F-13 · Nit · Alternatives, "The contents API" and "No way back at all" · "the sha, the base64 and the no-change comparison by hand" is a list of nouns where a reason should be. "Rejected as the whole answer" is unclear: say what part was kept.
- F-14 · Nit · Alternatives, "One `release.yml`…" · "the file would hold more conditions than jobs" is an unmeasured comparison that reads like rhetoric. Name the conditions or drop the phrase.
- F-15 · Nit · Preamble, line 3, and Acceptance criteria, line 235 · "reviewed by six reviewers with no context on 2026-11-12" and "earlier drafts used AC-1 to AC-38, and every criterion below was recorded again after the review" are process history that tells the builder nothing. Keep only the counter note if the engine needs it.
- F-16 · Nit · Acceptance criteria, AC-56 · One criterion bundles four documents and more than fifteen separate checks, and the "(observed)" marker is never explained. A reviewer cannot tell which part failed. Split it per document and define "observed".
- F-17 · Nit · Cost and duration · "leave the suite green" is jargon. Say "with `bun run check` passing".
