## Findings
- F-1 · Major · spec.md:54 · "git runs isolated: AC-2, AC-8, AC-9" lists the wrong criteria: AC-9 covers the author name and e-mails, and AC-8 covers missing git, leases and the timeout; the isolation checks are in AC-2 and AC-15. A reader running the Phase C midway check would test the author fallback, mark isolation as proven, and miss the `.git/config` refusal in AC-15.
- F-2 · Major · spec.md:194 · "A deleted entry file is staged with `git rm`" conflicts with the Non-Goal at spec.md:26 ("No deletion of entries through the engine") and with D-5. A reader who builds from the Non-Goal will make `adopt` refuse or skip hand deletions, and a reader who builds from line 194 will commit them. The spec has to say that adopt records a deletion made by hand and that this is not a deletion through the engine.
- F-3 · Major · spec.md:237 · "at most 5,000 files per folder (the newest by name beyond the cap are indexed and the count left out recorded in `over_cap`...)" cannot be followed. Names are slugs and UUIDs, which have no time order, so "newest by name" means nothing, and "beyond the cap are indexed" says the reverse of what a cap does. A reader cannot tell which 5,000 files make it into the index.
- F-4 · Major · spec.md:237 · "when git is missing the dirty check is skipped and the repository row says so" refers to a field that the `knowledge_repos` schema at spec.md:231 does not have. A reader will either add an unlisted column or drop the signal, and `doctor` and `rebuild` (spec.md:297) depend on that signal.
- F-5 · Major · spec.md:237 · "An entry whose `id` ... duplicates another's in the repository is indexed with a problem under the id `dup:<slug>`" does not say which of the two files keeps the real id. If the winner depends on listing order, AC-7's "the rebuilt rows equal the earlier ones" can fail between two rebuilds. The same gap applies to proposals with "present in two files".
- F-6 · Major · spec.md:254 · "It never opens the index" contradicts the same paragraph's "`moduleSchemaState` through `openExisting`", which opens the database. A reader will either drop the too-new schema warning or break the "never opens" rule. The sentence needs to say exactly what doctor may open.
- F-7 · Nit · spec.md:7 · "Obsidian notes that no coding agent can read" is an absolute claim that is false as written: these are Markdown files that any agent with file access can read. The checkable fact is that no agent is given them or pointed at them.
- F-8 · Nit · spec.md:7 · "never authoritative" is jargon that is never defined. The reader has to guess what makes a record authoritative.
- F-9 · Nit · spec.md:14 · "enforced on every entry" overstates the rule: a hand-edited entry with an unknown area is indexed as a draft with a problem (spec.md:102, spec.md:217), not refused. "Enforced on every write through the engine" is the accurate claim.
- F-10 · Nit · spec.md:15 · "ADR 0019 ruling F2" is cited and "module-owned migration namespace" is used before either is explained; the explanation only comes under The index (spec.md:221).
- F-11 · Nit · spec.md:16 · "A person's channel" uses "channel" as jargon. Its meaning (the entry point that decides which writer is passed) only appears at spec.md:158.
- F-12 · Nit · spec.md:52 · "one journey session", "by the gate" and "the plan's nine phases (24 tasks...)" use crew terms and a plan this spec does not define. "Phase C" and "Phase D" on spec.md:54 are also undefined here, so a new reader cannot say what each phase contains.
- F-13 · Nit · spec.md:52-53 · The Effort and Duration lines repeat each other ("one journey session of about four to six hours" and "One night session on the journey"), and neither says whether the extra hour for gates and reviewers is inside the four to six hours.
- F-14 · Nit · spec.md:102 · "A file the parser cannot read whole" also covers validation failures (an unknown `kind`, an `area` outside the taxonomy, a file over 256 KiB) that are not parse failures. "Read whole" hides that two different checks are involved.
- F-15 · Nit · spec.md:116 · "machine metadata nobody edits by hand" (repeated at spec.md:267 as "nobody edits it") is contradicted by spec.md:194, where `adopt` handles "`knowledge.json` changed by hand". The claim cannot be proven and the spec plans for the opposite case.
- F-16 · Nit · spec.md:118 · "else of its `key`" uses a project field that this spec never defines. The reader does not know which column or value is hashed.
- F-17 · Nit · spec.md:118 · "takes the one matched by `remote:`, else the newest by `createdAt`" does not cover two manifests that both match by `remote:`. Also, "is reported" does not say where.
- F-18 · Nit · spec.md:124 · "taking its environment (`PATH` included) from the store" is vague: "the store" can mean the store object, its settings or its directory.
- F-19 · Nit · spec.md:134 · "so no two operations wait on each other" describes a deadlock that cannot happen here, because a held lease refuses the operation instead of queueing it (spec.md:299). The fixed order prevents a partial claim, not waiting.
- F-20 · Nit · spec.md:145 · `requireGit` is used as a step before it is defined. Its behaviour is only given in prose at spec.md:129, under another name.
- F-21 · Nit · spec.md:153 · "fingerprint" is recorded at step 10, but it is only defined at spec.md:237.
- F-22 · Nit · spec.md:156 · "(two names never share an address)" cannot be proven: two names with the same slug share an address when the first 8 hex characters (32 bits) of their SHA-256 collide. "Practically never" or the collision odds would be accurate.
- F-23 · Nit · spec.md:158 · "cannot tell a caller's nature" is vague wording. The fact is that the store cannot tell whether the caller is a person or an agent.
- F-24 · Nit · spec.md:166 · "may carry a token" is ambiguous in a document about parsers and IDs. The meaning is a secret such as an API token.
- F-25 · Nit · spec.md:190 · The accept and reject rules sit in one paragraph of about 300 words, with nested parentheses that each carry a separate refusal. A numbered list like the person's write (spec.md:144-154) would let a reader check each refusal against AC-5, AC-15 and AC-18.
- F-26 · Nit · spec.md:217 · `addArea(store, root, ...)` takes a `root` parameter whose type is never given, while every other function takes `ref`. The reader does not know whether `root` is a scope, a path or a handle.
- F-27 · Nit · spec.md:223 · "a leaf" is jargon (a leaf module in the import graph). "So a hook pays nothing for it" sits awkwardly beside spec.md:224 and spec.md:311, where the module pass costs one query on every open.
- F-28 · Nit · spec.md:224 · "a quick lookup" uses "quick" as a judgement where the fact ("one query") is already in the sentence. "The withdrawn module's two columns" does not name the two columns.
- F-29 · Nit · spec.md:263 · "without a process" is shorthand for "without spawning git". Without that context, a reader cannot follow the reason the alternative was rejected.
- F-30 · Nit · spec.md:268 · The lock-file alternative is rejected because "it is swept by `git add -A`", but this design never runs `git add -A` (spec.md:140 stages named paths only), so the reason does not apply to the design as written.
- F-31 · Nit · spec.md:243-248 · `--scope` has a stated default only for `area`. `write`, `accept`, `reject`, `adopt` and `list` say "as above" or "…" and give no default, so whether the flag is required is left to the builder.
- F-32 · Nit · spec.md:276 · "`slugify` and the slug rule behave as specified" is a criterion that defers to the text instead of naming checkable cases. The AC's malformed cases also leave out the 256 KiB file from spec.md:102.
- F-33 · Nit · spec.md:288 · "logged under a knowledge scope" reuses "scope", which this spec defines as workspace/project/shared, for the log key `knowledge.<operation>` from spec.md:130.
- F-34 · Nit · spec.md:289 · AC-15's list of refused keys leaves out the `filter` section, which spec.md:128 refuses and justifies at length.
- F-35 · Nit · spec.md:289-293 · The criteria run AC-13, AC-15, AC-16, AC-17, AC-18, AC-14. The superseded AC-14 at the end breaks the order a reader scans by.
- F-36 · Nit · spec.md:307 · "objection O-1, advisory" cites an objection that this spec never defines or locates.
- F-37 · Nit · spec.md:313 · "three to nine git calls" does not match its own breakdown: 3, plus 3 for each of up to two repositories created, plus `--version`, is 10, and the `config --global user.name` lookup adds one more.
- F-38 · Nit · spec.md:313 · "normally well under a second in all" is an unmeasured performance claim made two sentences after "Not yet measured". It should be dropped or marked as an expectation.
- F-39 · Nit · spec.md:314 · "no caller-supplied name with a separator or a dot reaches the file system" is broader than the design: `--body-file <path>` is a caller-supplied path. The claim should be limited to names inside the repository.
- F-40 · Nit · spec.md:316-318 · "Out of scope" repeats the job of Non-Goals (spec.md:19-28) in a second section. A reader has to check both to learn what is excluded.
