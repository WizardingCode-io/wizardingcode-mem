## Findings
- F-1 · Major · spec.md:251-252, Acceptance criteria · AC-14 is still in the list, marked "superseded by AC-18", and placed before AC-15. AC-14 says that when an adopt's commit fails, "every path it staged is restored from HEAD". That is the opposite of the design (line 112, line 161, AC-18), which writes the person's bytes back. Someone working from the criteria list could build or test AC-14 and wipe out a person's uncommitted hand edits. A replaced criterion should not stay in the list as something to meet.
- F-2 · Major · spec.md:3, 32, 94 and throughout · D-1 to D-11 justify more than twenty rulings, but the spec never says what any of them is. It only says they are "recorded on the item with their defaults". AC-12 asks the ADR to record "D-1 to D-11 as rulings", and that ADR cannot be written from this document. "D-1 refined by the lenses" (line 94) adds a second term the spec never defines, "the lenses".
- F-3 · Major · spec.md:32, Open Questions · "The eleven decisions have recorded defaults; the validator applies them where nobody watches." Nothing defines "the validator", and "where nobody watches" is a figure of speech, not a fact. The sentence also leaves open whether D-1 to D-11 are decided or just defaulted until someone rules. A reader cannot tell whether the design that rests on them is final.
- F-4 · Major · spec.md:157, Proposals, `acceptProposal` · An accept runs "steps 5 to 8 of a person's write with `baseVersion` = `base_version`". This leaves out step 2 (validation). A hand-edited proposal (the sentence itself says one "can carry any string") with a bad `area` or `kind` would then be written as an entry. It also leaves the new-entry case undefined. A new entry's proposal has `base_version: 0` (line 141), but step 6 (line 121) treats a new entry as "no `baseVersion`" and otherwise compares `baseVersion !== current.version` on a file that does not exist. An implementer has to guess both behaviours.
- F-5 · Major · spec.md:141, proposal example · `base_version: 2          # 0 for a new entry` puts a trailing comment inside the file format. Line 80 says `#` is a comment only at the start of a line, so this line would parse as a value that is not a whole number. Someone who treats the example as the format either adds inline comments to the parser or writes a fixture the parser marks with a problem. The note belongs in prose.
- F-6 · Major · spec.md:204 vs spec.md:250 · Rebuild lists "visible regular files only, `O_NOFOLLOW`", which means symlinks are skipped. AC-13 says "a symlinked entry file is listed with a problem". The two statements contradict each other, and the AC test will fail against whichever one gets built. "Visible" is also never defined (no leading dot?).
- F-7 · Major · spec.md:78, The entry file, slug · The slug is called "a name that may change". But an entry is addressed by its slug (line 110), and no operation in the spec changes one. Either a rename exists and is unspecified, or it does not exist and the hedge is false. A reader cannot tell whether to build a rename.
- F-8 · Major · spec.md:217, Doctor · The check is "reading files and `.git` by hand, never the index", yet it then warns "when `git status --porcelain` lists changes", which is a git process. Someone building doctor cannot tell whether doctor may spawn git, and that matters for the 2.32 or missing-git case it is meant to report.
- F-9 · Major · Whole document, Heilmeier questions 6 and 7 · Nothing states the cost (people, effort) or how long the part takes. spec-heilmeier.md requires both. Without them, a reader judging the part cannot tell whether eighteen criteria and a new storage layer fit in one part of a six-part journey.
- F-10 · Nit · spec.md:128 · "the machine's global `git config --global user.name` (one call allowed the user's configuration, cached per process)". "global" appears twice. The parenthesis is garbled: it seems to mean "the one call allowed to read the user's configuration". It also needs to be stated as the exception to line 103 ("Nothing of the person's git setup … runs here").
- F-11 · Nit · spec.md:130 · "Which writer a caller passes is the channel's" is opaque. "this guarantee" points back to a guarantee that is never named in that section. Line 268 does name it ("The guarantee is for writes through the engine").
- F-12 · Nit · spec.md:134 · The quota has two limits (5 per entry, 200 per repository), but the one quoted message only covers the repository limit. Nothing says what message the per-entry refusal gives.
- F-13 · Nit · spec.md:191, 196 vs spec.md:233, 240 · The old module is called "the withdrawn module" in some places and "the archived module" in others. Two names for one thing, and "archived" also means an entry status (line 73).
- F-14 · Nit · spec.md:194 · "nothing here has a foreign key or a trigger on a core table except `knowledge_repos.project_id`, which is a plain integer, not a constraint". The exception is then said not to be one. Better: "no table here has a foreign key or trigger on a core table; `knowledge_repos.project_id` is a plain integer".
- F-15 · Nit · spec.md:191 · "finds nothing pending on almost every open": "almost every" is vague. The fact behind it is "every open after the first migration".
- F-16 · Nit · spec.md:7 · "a person's acceptance between an agent and the truth": "the truth" is a figure of speech. The concrete fact is that an agent's change reaches an entry only after a person accepts it.
- F-17 · Nit · spec.md:15 · "now with its real case" does not say what the case is. The plain fact is "the first module that uses it".
- F-18 · Nit · spec.md:3 · "the concept after six reviewers" and "the user's brief … in the main checkout" give no path, so the reader cannot check either source.
- F-19 · Nit · spec.md:43 · "the pinned snapshots" is used before it is defined. AC-11 (line 248) is the first place that names the files.
- F-20 · Nit · spec.md:117 · "the taxonomy of the repository's root" uses "root" before it is defined (line 119 explains it in a parenthesis, line 165 in prose).
- F-21 · Nit · spec.md:124 vs spec.md:112 · Step 9 repeats the failure rule that line 112 already states. If one copy is edited, the two will drift apart.
- F-22 · Nit · spec.md:226 · "a decision is knowledge about the knowledge" reads as an aphorism. The fact that carries the reason is the second half: "the index should find it without a process".
- F-23 · Nit · spec.md:259 · "Writes refuse with a clear message": "clear" is a judgement. Line 105 quotes the message, so point to it. "skipped and said" does not say where it is said (stdout? the line format?).
- F-24 · Nit · spec.md:273 · "each about 30 to 80 ms on the author's machine; acceptable for a person's or a tool's write". The figure has no machine, no git version and no method. "Acceptable" has no bound to measure against.
- F-25 · Nit · spec.md:274 · "nothing with a separator or a dot reaches the file system" is false as written. `entries/.<slug>.md.tmp` (line 123) and every `.md` path contain dots. The claim means caller-supplied names.
- F-26 · Nit · spec.md:238 vs spec.md:80 · AC-1's malformed cases leave out two that line 80 lists: an empty `title` and a file over 256 KiB.
- F-27 · Nit · spec.md:217 · "the database may be older" does not say older than what. The fact is "its knowledge schema may be older or newer than this binary's".
