## Returns
- R-1 · docs/adr/0027-sync-client.md:102 · "Known limits" does not record the one data gap the delivery leaves open. The data reviewer marked it "not answerable" in all six rounds (review/data.findings.md:12, and the same entry in review/data.round1.md:26 through data.round6.md:12). Nothing measures how long the write lock is held in three cases:
  - **Server removal.** `share server remove` deletes every received memory, ledger row and copy of the server in one transaction.
  - **First full sweep** over many entries.
  - **Restore of an old backup.** `restoreBackup` holds `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on the live store (src/backup/backup.ts:318) while `openStoreFile` upgrades the copy, which creates the new indexes on the copy's `memories`, and while `carryIntoCopy` runs (src/backup/backup.ts:324-330).
  
  `scripts/proof/upgrade-copy.ts` measures only the first open. The 50 ms bar (tests/integration/sync-received.test.ts:911-1014) covers only a pull chunk of 50 rows. A hook that waits longer than its busy timeout fails open, so the cost is lost context, not lost data. Still, the delivery must say what is left. Add one sentence under "Known limits": these three write-lock holds are unmeasured on a large store, and they show up as hooks failing open while the operation runs. Name the remedy, for example run `remove` and `--restore` outside a session.

It is not in the SQL file. Tests: the part-5 upgrade at sync-schema.test.ts:300, and the forced failure leaving sharing 1 and knowledge 7 at :338.
- **Reversibility.** The migrations are forward-only, as the core's are. The first opener's `VACUUM INTO` copy (src/store/db.ts:330, only when a recorded schema has a pending migration) is the way back. ADR 0027:102 records what a rollback to an older binary does.
- **AC-91, the sharing v1 to v2 copy, is measured and recorded.** `evidence.json:1013` gives the fixture: 66.8 MB, 40,000 memories, 2,000 entries, 500 ledger rows. The first open took 124 ms, including a 65.9 MB copy and the new index, against the 10 s bar. The script builds its fixture through the real migration lists (scripts/proof/upgrade-copy.ts:30-33), never on the live store.
- **Pull transactions.** Each row applies in its own short transaction. The lease is checked inside every write (`guarded`, src/sync/state.ts:43-49), and files and vectors are written outside it.
- **Round 3's defect is closed.** `deletePendingRowsUpTo` (src/sync/ledger.ts:439-452) is called both from the feed (src/sync/inbound.ts:374) and from the hourly re-apply (inbound.ts:494). An older failing row can no longer come back over a newer revision. Tests: tests/integration/sync-inbound.test.ts:761, :810, :912.
- **Restore transactions.** The carry replaces the eight tables wholesale in one `withWrite` on the copy (src/backup/backup.ts:211-247). It squares received memories with the carried ledger (:180-202), resets the levels, clears the hold, authorised and `sync.*` keys, sets the restore hold and `snapshot_due`, and switches to `journal_mode = DELETE` before the rename (:332-352). A leftover `-wal` refuses the rename. tests/integration/sync-restore.test.ts:267, :506, :628 and :708 cover the carry, the memory reconcile, WAL and fault handling, and the v1 copy upgraded with `upgradeBackup: false`.
- **Deleting a received memory** (src/store/memories.ts:239-263) resets `superseded_by` and status first, then removes the provenance and the `received` ledger row, then the row itself, whose deletion cascades.
- **Build note, not a return.** `deletePendingRowsUpTo` filters on `uid` with no index; the key is `(server_id, seq)`. That is fine while pending rows stay few. Add an index if the set-aside ever grows.
