- R-1 · .crew/work/2026-11-10-002-part-6-of-the-knowledge-and-team-journey/spec.md:117 · The push base is "the ledger's `sent` revision and hash for the uid". A teammate's revision applied to an own entry (spec.md:125) writes only a `received` row, so the sent row keeps the old revision. The person's next edit is then pushed on that stale base. The server answers `stale`, and `current.hash` (the teammate's) is not in the sent history, so every edit made after a teammate's revision becomes a false conflict under spec.md:119. Fix: the base is the newer, by revision, of the uid's `sent` and `received` rows for that server, or the apply also moves the sent row's base. Add an AC-19 case at spec.md:181: a teammate's revision is applied, the person edits, and the edit is `applied` with no conflict.
- R-2 · .crew/work/2026-11-10-002-part-6-of-the-knowledge-and-team-journey/spec.md:123 · The alias hand-over contradicts itself and can lose rows. "A changed group starts from 0 with a snapshot" and "joins its group and the group's cursor is reset to the snapshot's `next`" cannot both hold, because adding an alias changes the group's hash. And seq is global: if the group's cursor is moved up to the one-alias snapshot's `next`, the rows of the group's other aliases between the old cursor and that `next` are never pulled. State one rule. Either the group cursor after the hand-over is `min(group cursor, snapshot next)`, re-applied idempotently, or the changed group re-snapshots and the one-alias snapshot is dropped. Prove it in AC-21 (spec.md:183) with a row of another alias in the group written during the new alias's snapshot.
- R-3 · .crew/work/2026-11-10-002-part-6-of-the-knowledge-and-team-journey/plan.md:93 · The 50 ms write-lock bound of AC-20 (spec.md:182) is to be measured "with the fake clock stepping through `busy_timeout` reads". SQLite's lock and `busy_timeout` run on real time, so an injected clock cannot observe how long the lock is held, and the criterion would pass whatever the hold. Measure on the real clock: time `performance.now()` around the largest chunk's `withWrite` (50 received memories and 50 entry rows with their search rows, at the payload cap), and have a second connection with `busy_timeout` 50 run `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` during the chunk and succeed. Keep the fake clock for the sync's own timers only.
- R-4 · .crew/work/2026-11-10-002-part-6-of-the-knowledge-and-team-journey/spec.md:146 · When the carry fails after the rename, the stated remedy is `share server rejoin`, but rejoin needs a listed server (spec.md:95), and the restored copy may not have the row. Even with a rejoin, the sent rows and sent hashes are gone, so every own entry is re-pushed on base 0, answered `stale` with a hash outside the history, and becomes a conflict. Fix: the carry reads from the kept `<db>.replaced-<time>` file (which still holds the live rows), and a `meta` mark retries it until it completes (at `openDb`'s next writer or the sync's start, before any sweep). `status` and `doctor` show it while it is owed. plan.md:103 (task 17) adds the failure case to AC-22.
