## Findings
- F-1 · Major · spec.md:263 (Non-functional requirements, Latency) · "a change reaches a connected machine in under 5 s" is stated with no conditions, but the same sentence names a fallback that breaks it. The 2 s tick plus the server's "5 s poll" when the in-process signal is missing already comes to more than 5 s, and the only proof is in-process ("the real network adding its round trips"). A reader would treat 5 s as a guarantee on a real network and build alerts or expectations on it. Must change: limit the claim to what is proven (in-process, with the server's signal wake) and give the bound for the poll fallback and for a real network, or say they are not measured. Line 29 (Goals) should carry the same limits.
- F-2 · Major · spec.md:258 (Rollback, first bullet) · "its `history replace-imports` already spares `team:…` (ADR 0025's own narrowing, which this part implements) only if it was built with it (0.7.0 of part 5 was not …)" contradicts itself. "Already spares" says older binaries protect received memories, while "which this part implements" says no older binary does. A reader judging the downgrade risk could decide that 0.6.0 is safe and run the command, which deletes received memories. Must change: say plainly which released binaries, if any, spare `team:…` rows, and that every binary older than this part does not.
- F-3 · Nit · spec.md:263 · "the writer's 2 s tick" names the wrong owner. Line 126 gives the 2 s tick to the sync process, not to the writer. Must change: "the sync's 2 s tick".
- F-4 · Nit · spec.md:30, 171, 180, 249 · "the prompt floor", "a brief" and "the brief's slots" carry the design, but the Terms section does not define them, so a fresh reader cannot see what the third-of-a-brief cap and the one-entry floor cap limit. Must change: add both to Terms, or point to the ADR 0024 section that defines them.
- F-5 · Nit · spec.md:248 · "burning the device's buckets" is jargon: "buckets" (the server's rate-limit buckets) is defined nowhere in this document. Must change: name the limit, for example "using up the device's rate limits (ADR 0026 D-15)".
- F-6 · Nit · spec.md:140 · `bytes / 18,641 per second` is a bare constant. It appears to be 64 MiB an hour (line 251), but the text does not say so, so a reader cannot check it or update it when the limit changes. Must change: write it as derived from the server's 64 MiB-an-hour limit.
