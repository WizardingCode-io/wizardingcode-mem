## Findings
- F-1 · Major · concept.md "The hypothesis" 2-3 · The concept never says where the updater lives. Its parts are scattered: a CLI command, a `meta` lease, a hook spawn copied from `src/sync/spawn.ts`, and download code modelled on `src/embed/download.ts`. Nothing ties them to the per-release, per-product facts (repository slug, asset name, data dir, env prefix). A second `wizardingcode-<thing>` product would copy all of it. It needs to be a product-agnostic `src/update/` module with those facts injected. It is not an engine module: ADR 0019 modules contribute context and do not manage the process.
- F-2 · Major · concept.md:19 vs src/embed/download.ts · "Verifies the sha256 as `src/embed/download.ts` does" means a second copy of the part-file, streamed-hash and stale-sweep logic. CLAUDE.md calls the embed download "the feature's only network access", and this item breaks that sentence. In two years the two copies will have drifted apart. Extract one verified downloader that both use, or the promised signature swap ("behind one function") will be made in one place only.
- F-3 · Major · concept.md:23 + ADR 0029 "One version order" · Today three `older()` implementations are kept equal by a table. Adding five version readers in four languages or contexts (TS, generated JS twice, two shell scripts) makes two parallel equal-by-table families. Every rule change then means edits in up to eight places and two fixture tables. The concept should say which copies are generated from one source (as `OLDER_JS` is), and keep the hand-written copies (`scripts/install.sh`) to the irreducible minimum.
- F-4 · Major · concept.md:23 · "Exit 0 within 10 seconds" puts a timeout into each of five readers, and two of them are POSIX shell, where `timeout` is not portable (macOS has no `timeout` by default). That clause is the one most likely to differ between readers on a real machine. A shared table cannot test it, because the table checks answers, not timing.
- F-5 · Major · concept.md:22 · The hook-contract compatibility test has to run the hook invocations of every shipped generated script against the current binary, and it grows by one fixture per release forever. Nothing says when an old plugin version may be dropped. Without a stated support window (for example the last N releases, or any version with the same `CONTRACT`), this test becomes the largest, slowest and least-understood suite in the repository.
- F-6 · Major · concept.md:22 · "The plugin's pin becomes a floor" quietly turns every hook argument, event name and MCP flag ever shipped into a permanent public API of the binary. That is a one-way promise. It needs its own ADR line with a deprecation rule, not a test alone.
- F-7 · Major · concept.md:20 vs src/sync/context.ts:33 · Point 4 says it "adds no new state", but nothing in the store records a binary version today. The sync's takeover works by comparing the lease owner `sync-<pid>-<version>`. Having the MCP server and the viewer end themselves on "the store's recorded binary version" needs a new `meta` key, a writer, and an answer to who writes it. That is new state, in the same keyspace as the leases.
- F-8 · Major · concept.md:18 + src/store/meta.ts · `update.lease`, `update.latest` and `update.checked_at` live in the store's `meta`, but the thing being updated is `<data dir>/bin`, which sits apart from the store (`WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_STORE_DIR`, ADR 0010). Several consequences follow. A store move or a `backup --restore` resets or carries the check state. Two data dirs pointing at one store share a lease for two binaries. A store whose schema the old binary refuses (`SchemaTooNewError`) also blocks that binary's update check, which is the very case where the update matters most. The concept also does not say whether the new lease blocks move, restore and compact, as `embed.*` does.
- F-9 · Nit · concept.md:18,21 · The `meta` keyspace already holds a dozen unprefixed lease names and dotted keys (`sync.lease`, `rules.cursor`, `names.missing:…`) with no registry. Adding `update.*` deepens the risk of key collisions and orphaned keys. Name the keys in one exported constant block beside `SYNC_LEASE`.
- F-10 · Nit · concept.md:7,21 · `WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_UPDATE=off|check` plus an unwritten default (on) is a three-value switch, while every existing switch is binary (`=off`). The concept should state the default value's name, how `<data dir>/env` and the viewer's Settings show it, and what an unknown value means (fail to `check`, not to replace). Without that, the third value invites a fourth (`pre`, `channel=`) with no rule for it.
- F-11 · Major · concept.md:19,26-28 · "A binary that updates itself" is a promise that is hard to take back. Once shipped, every installed client keeps fetching `releases/latest` from `WizardingCode-io/wizardingcode-mem` and running what it finds there. Moving or renaming the repository, changing an asset name or the `checksums.txt` format, or wanting a kill switch for a bad release all become compatibility constraints on old clients. ADR 0029's own limit says assets can be replaced between release and distribution, and signatures are out of scope. Before this ships, the concept should define a server-side "do not advance past" signal and freeze the asset naming in an ADR.
- F-12 · Major · concept.md:19 · Auto-replacing the binary undercuts ADR 0029's only way back (`install --force`). The next daily check moves the binary forward again, and an older binary cannot open a store the newer one migrated. A person who rolls back has no supported way to stay back short of `UPDATE=off`, and the concept does not name that as the rollback path.
- F-13 · Major · concept.md "The hypothesis" 1-8 · Eight points in one item mix a feature (points 1-3, 5, 8), a cross-cutting refactor (point 7, imported from item 005), a lifecycle change to `mcp` and `ui` (point 4) and a permanent compatibility policy (point 6). Cut it into: (a) check and tell (1, 2, 5, 8, `check` as the default); (b) one version-reading rule (7), on its own; (c) replace plus the processes ending themselves (3, 4), behind a policy for point 6. That way (a) can ship and be lived with before the hard-to-reverse promise in (c) is made.
