## Findings
- F-1 · Nit · spec.md "Push", the paragraph "For a memory `send`: …" (line 185) · The entry send's rules are a numbered list in a binding order (1 Shape to 11 Write), but the memory send's rules, which also have a binding order (shape, size, hash, secret, then idempotency, then storage, then replace-or-stale), are packed into one long sentence joined by semicolons, and the withdraw rules (line 187) are the same. That makes the order hard to read and hard to check against AC-34. Give the memory send and the withdraw numbered lists like the entry send's.
- F-2 · Nit · spec.md "Push", first paragraph (line 169) · The cross-references `("Pull")` and `("Restore detection")` don't match a heading exactly. The heading is "Pull, the feed, the wait, the instance", and "Restore detection" is a bold run-in paragraph inside that section, not a heading. Elsewhere the spec quotes headings exactly ("The command and the data directory", "Compatibility", "Risks and mitigations"). Name the full heading, or the heading plus the paragraph.
- F-3 · Nit · spec.md "Pull, the feed, the wait, the instance", the paragraph beginning **Snapshots.** (line 195) · The bold run-in **Moves.** sits inside the Snapshots paragraph. The other run-ins in this section (**Cursors and restores.**, **Waiting for the next change**, **Restore detection**) each start their own paragraph. Start **Moves.** on its own paragraph.
- F-4 · Nit · spec.md "Decisions", the table's fourth column header "Clause superseded by a later decision" (line 64) · Most cells in this column quote a clause from the concept that the row itself supersedes, for example D-2 "salted", D-3 "written to a 0600 file" and D-17 "`serve.lock` with a pid". Only some cite a later D. The header misnames what the column holds. Rename it, for example "Clause of the concept it supersedes".
- F-5 · Nit · spec.md "Alternatives considered", the bullet "Entries keyed by the client's uid" (line 246) · It cites D-20 as its ruling. D-20 is server-side validation, and the UUID-as-key ruling is in D-8 and the architecture's `entries` entity, not in D-20. Cite the decision that actually rules it, or just ADR 0024 D-8.
- F-6 · Nit · spec.md "Acceptance criteria", line 267-268 · Two blank lines separate the id note from the list. Every other section uses one. Remove one.
- F-7 · Nit · spec.md AC-31 (line 270) against "Proposed architecture" · AC-31 pins `/v1/health` as 503 under 100 MiB free and 200 above, and says it "carries no version". No architecture section states either rule. They appear only in AC-31 and in passing under "Risks and mitigations" ("health fails under 100 MiB") and "Non-functional requirements". A reader building from the architecture alone would miss the 503. State the health route's answer and its disk floor once under "Limits, rate limits, logs" or "The command and the data directory".
