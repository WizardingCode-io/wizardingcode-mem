## Findings
- F-1 · Major · §6 `stripHost`, `<agent-message>` / `<task-notification>` / `<system-reminder>` rows · An opening tag with no closing tag removes everything from the tag to the end of the text. The built code does this: `block()` in `src/distill/noise.ts:17` ends with `(?:</tag>|$)`, and with no `m` flag `$` means the end of the text. So an answer that only mentions the tag in prose or in backticks (for example "the hook now strips `<system-reminder>` before…") loses every sentence after the mention. That is likely in this repository and in any other project that works on hosts. The spec anchored `host-frame` for exactly this reason but left the block removal unanchored. The proof only reports quoted sentences lost inside removed text, and the design has no guard such as a line start or no backticks. A builder will ship the behaviour as it stands.
- F-2 · Major · §11 Metrics, "Lost" row, and §2 goal 3 · The target is zero: "version 2 loses no turn version 1 kept". Version 1 is re-run live, not read from what it stored, and TypeSafe answers are probabilities from a model that can vary between runs. The ~76 knowledge or mixed turns expected in B (38% of 200, going by §1) are few enough that a re-run of version 1 against itself could already lose one or more. The spec gives no measure of run-to-run noise (for example version 1 against a second version 1 run), no confidence bound and no tolerance. The merge gate can then fail or pass by chance, and "accept a named loss" becomes the usual exit rather than an exception.
- F-3 · Major · §11 Samples and labels, label check · The labels can be wrong in 3 of 20 checked (15%) and still be accepted. That is the same size as the record target (≤15%) and far looser than the zero-loss target. A check of 20 cannot tell a 5% labeller error rate from a 20% one. The spec does not say why 20 is enough or what error it tolerates against each target. If the labeller and the judge are both large models, their errors may be correlated, and the proof would then overstate version 2.
- F-4 · Major · §11 Metrics, "Title is knowledge" and "Lost" · Both metrics compare sentences by exact equality: the title's full sentence must equal a quoted sentence. The labeller quotes "verbatim" from the turn as it reads it, but candidates come from `candidates()`, which splits sentences its own way, drops fragments under 20 characters or 3 words, and now runs after `stripHost`. The spec does not say whether the brief gives the labeller the candidate list to quote from. Without that, a quote and a candidate that differ by one boundary, a markdown mark or whitespace count as a miss. Both metrics would then be biased downward, and calibration would lower thresholds in response.
- F-5 · Major · §12 risk 1, §13 Rollback, D-1 · Knowledge that version 2 drops in daily use can only be recovered by re-distilling within 90 days (`compact`). The cleanup that would do it (D-1) is not designed. Nothing extends retention for turns version 2 skipped, and there is no measure of quality after the merge, so the only defence is the check before the merge. Also, `turns` has no column for the judge version or for when a turn was distilled (checked in `0001_init.sql` and `0004_history.sql`). A skipped turn therefore does not record which version, judge (heuristic or TypeSafe) or filter skipped it, so the cleanup cannot select the turns version 2 skipped. Sample S has the same gap: "turns version 1 skipped" also contains turns the heuristic skipped and turns skipped after a failure, and the spec does not say how they are told apart.
- F-6 · Major · §9 and D-6 · The heuristic version 2 is checked only for loss, and only on sample B. B is drawn from memories that TypeSafe judged (`judge = 'typesafe'`), not from what keyless installations see. The share stored from record turns (≤15%) and the title share are never measured for the heuristic. For keyless users, and for the drain whenever TypeSafe fails, the goals "a record stores nothing" and "the title is knowledge" have no check at all.
- F-7 · Major · §7 `knowledge_<i>` / `title`, D-5 · Every candidate adds one `knowledge_<i>` question that repeats the long version 2 criteria, and the `title` choice gets one more option. The spec does not say whether the number of candidates per turn has a cap. D-5 bounds the mean on B only, so a long answer with dozens of sentences has no bound on its token count, its latency or the request-size limits of TypeSafe. The spec states no ceiling for a single turn and no behaviour when one is hit.
- F-8 · Major · §11 Cost; Heilmeier Q6 · The spec has no price: it contains the literal placeholder "[source?] for TypeSafe's price per token". It also gives no cost for production: D-5 allows up to twice the tokens on every judged turn for good, but the spec never states that in money or against quota. It is also unclear whether calibration needs new TypeSafe requests ("plus re-runs for calibration on A"). Changing a threshold only re-runs policy code on verdicts already received, so the cost depends on whether verdicts are cached, and the spec does not say.
- F-9 · Major · whole document; Heilmeier Q7 · The spec never says how long the work will take. There is no estimate for the build, the labelling of ~500 turns, or the third review round (D-7).
- F-10 · Major · §5 step 7, §11 "What runs" · `measure` stops at the draft, so consolidation is never measured. Version 2 drafts have new titles and bodies that are compared with ~3,900 version 1 memories. The merge, insert and replace-on-contradiction decisions were tuned on version 1 output. A version 2 draft that "contradicts" a version 1 memory could replace a memory that held knowledge, and none of the proof's numbers would show it. The spec names this neither as a risk nor as a limit.
- F-11 · Major · §8 body rule and §5 step 3 · Candidates come from the prompt as well as the answer, and finding 1 names "the user's own prompts" as a common source of records. The title rule covers a title taken from the answer (`Context:`), but nothing in §7 or §8 says how a sentence from the prompt competes for the title or the five body slots. A prompt that says "aprovo, merge it and close W4" can still be rated as knowledge, and the spec has no journey or test for a title taken from the prompt.
- F-12 · Nit · §11 Samples and labels, "The labels never enter the repository" · §1 records that the first labels were lost with a scratch directory. The spec still names no place to keep the labels, so the ADR's numbers cannot be reproduced and the same loss can happen again.
- F-13 · Nit · §11 Metrics, "Title is knowledge" denominator · The denominator is "memories stored from samples A or B", but A is the calibration sample and §11 says the ADR's numbers come from B. Reading this denominator as including A mixes the tuned sample into the reported result.
- F-14 · Nit · §1 · The store has 2,513 active distilled memories on 2026-10-14 and 3,941 on 2026-11-06, a 57% rise in 23 days. Nothing reconciles the two counts (for example a different filter, or imports or re-judging in between), and the reader cannot tell whether finding 2's 79% describes the same set of memories.
- F-15 · Nit · D-4, §12 "The labeller sees the author's project text" · By default the labeller is the runtime's own agent (a Claude Code or Codex model), so text from 59 projects goes to an outside model unless the author chooses otherwise. "The proof sends nothing itself" is true but hides this, and the spec should say plainly what the default sends.
- F-16 · Nit · §2; Heilmeier Q4 · The benefit to a later session (better briefs and retrieval from fewer records) is only implied. No metric of later effect is named, even as a measurement to take after the change.
