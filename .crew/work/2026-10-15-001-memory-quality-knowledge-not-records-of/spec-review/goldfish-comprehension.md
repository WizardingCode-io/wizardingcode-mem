## Findings

**What this proposes**
The change makes the mem store fewer, better memories from now on: knowledge a newcomer would act on, not records of what happened in a session. A pure filter runs before the judge and does two things. It removes the host's framing blocks, and it drops sentences that are never knowledge: host frame lines anchored at the start of a sentence, `Status:` labels and leaked `Title:`/`Body:` templates. If no candidate is left, the turn is skipped with no request. Version 2 of the TypeSafe questions names a fixed `reader` and asks a `knowledge_<i>` question per sentence, with criteria that say what is not knowledge. Version 2 of the heuristic judge penalises sentences that look like reports. The policy now requires the title to be a knowledge sentence and adds a `Context:` line only for prompts of 5 or more words. The import rejudge stays on the version 1 questions. Before merging, a proof labels samples A, B and S from a read-only copy of the store, measures version 1 against version 2, and calibrates two thresholds on A.

**How the existing system works, per this document**
Each finished turn (the user's prompt and the assistant's final answer) is queued. A background drain holds a lease and processes the queue. For each turn it removes the sentences of memories already injected into the session (`withoutNotes`), with no time bound today. It splits the rest into candidate sentences and sends one TypeSafe request, falling back to a local heuristic when TypeSafe fails or there is no key. The request asks for `worth`, `kind`, `importance`, a per-sentence `durable_<i>` and a `title` choice. `policy.ts` turns the answers into a draft, which is then inserted, merged or superseded against similar memories. Today a `worth` pass with one durable sentence is enough to store a memory. As a result 79% of TypeSafe memories sit at importance 4 and most are records of the session.

- F-1 · Major · Spec §7 importance table, row 3 vs plan Task 6 Step 3 (`criteria` of `importance`) · The two texts for level 3 differ. Spec: "a cause, a behaviour or a location they would not find by reading the code around it". Plan code: "...that takes real time to find". The plan says the spec wins and that the only known difference is in Task 6, yet it does not list this one. Someone building from the plan ships, records and measures wording that is not the one the spec approved.
- F-2 · Major · Spec §8 "The body: the five strongest knowledge sentences (`MAX_FACTS = 5`)... without the title" vs plan Task 3 `buildDraft` and its test "facts are the knowledge sentences, at most five" · The plan keeps the top 5 sentences and then removes the title, so the body holds only 4 facts whenever the title is among them (the test expects 4). The spec reads as five facts besides the title. A reader cannot tell which is intended, and whichever is wrong gets locked in by the test.
- F-3 · Major · Spec §11 "What runs" / plan Task 7 "Version 1 is the memory stored" · The spec says every TypeSafe request runs in both wordings "so both versions are measured on the same turns". A reader concludes that version 1's outcome is computed afresh. In the plan, version 1 is instead the memory stored months ago, made by another run with old notes handling and no filter, and it may have changed since. The wording-1 request is used only for its token count. The spec never says this. A reader of the spec alone would build or interpret the "lost" and "title is knowledge" baselines wrongly.
- F-4 · Major · Spec §11 Metrics and Calibration; plan Task 7 `render` (`titled`, `recordBase`) and Task 9 Steps 3–5 · Two inconsistencies. First, "Title is knowledge" is measured on memories from A and B, but A is the calibration sample. Second, the record share uses S, which is also measured during calibration (`--a ... --s ...`). The spec says "the numbers for the ADR come from sample B" and the plan says "B is never used to tune". Two of the three merge targets are therefore partly scored on data the thresholds were tuned on, and the ADR reports them as check numbers. The risk of overfitting is not visible.
- F-5 · Major · Spec §11 Calibration ("one lever at a time, in this order, each in steps of 0.05 and at most three steps") · The spec does not say what happens when levers conflict: records over 15% call for raising `KNOWLEDGE_THRESHOLD`, while lost turns call for lowering it. It also does not say whether "three steps" applies per lever or in total, or whether a later lever may undo an earlier one. Whoever runs Task 9 Step 4 can oscillate or stop at different points, so two runs would not pick the same thresholds.
- F-6 · Major · Spec D-6 and §11 "Lost, for the heuristic"; plan Task 7 (`h1` = `heuristicJudgeV1` through `draftTurn`) · The "heuristic version 1" reading runs through version 2's filter and version 2's policy (title must be knowledge, `Context:` rule). The spec does not state this limit.
