# The sharing policy: what may leave this machine

Part 4 of the knowledge and team work (journey 2026-11-08-001). Sources: the person's brief of 2026-11-08 (Q-3: "there can be projects, knowledge and memory I do not want to send to the server"); the mission ("the user chooses what is shared, and projects, knowledge areas and memories can be kept off the server"; "settings in `<data dir>/env` and viewer Settings"); part 1's spec and plan, `.crew/work/2026-11-08-003-knowledge-store-entries-as-markdown-with/` (the knowledge store, its index and its module migration namespace; its plan also adds `listRepos` and `runCliWith`'s `cwd`; waiting for the person's approval, and this part follows any change the person asks there); `concepts_brief.md` in this folder (the concept after the six brainstorm reviewers, `spec-review/brainstorm-goldfish-*.md`); the decisions D-1 to D-20 in `work.json`, summarised in the Decisions table, which is the binding text for the ADR; the objections O-1 to O-4 in `work.json` and `conclave.md`; the plan is `plan.md`.

Terms used throughout: the person is the owner of this machine and its data; the viewer is the local web app the binary serves; the dock is the viewer's 320-pixel detail panel; Across projects is the viewer's view of every project and their links; a TypeSafe judgement is the optional model call that classifies a memory, made only when a key is set (the judge is that classifier, TypeSafe or the heuristic; this part never calls it); the hook snapshots are the pinned texts the hooks inject (`tests/unit/hook-snapshots.test.ts`); parts 5 and 6 are the team server and the sync client of this journey; the boundary is the local user account: any process that account runs can change its shell, its files and its settings, and the policy protects what leaves this account's machine, not the account from itself.

## Problem

Parts 5 and 6 will move knowledge and memories to a team server. Before any of that exists, the person has to be able to say what may leave this machine and what never does: for each project, nothing, its knowledge, or its knowledge and its memories; for the workspace and the shared knowledge repositories, whether they leave at all; and for each knowledge area of each root, allowed to leave or private. Today the engine has no notion of leaving. The only text that leaves is a TypeSafe judgement. The viewer has a Collaboration card that promises a per-project opt-in nothing implements. If the policy is written after the sync, the first sync sends what the person never chose; and a level stored without a destination sends one project's knowledge to every server a later part configures.

Success is visible when:

- a fresh store lets nothing out;
- the person's three choices are stored and shown;
- one function answers `send`, `withdraw` or `keep` with a reason for any entry or memory and any destination;
- every change is logged and a widening is confirmed first;
- the hook snapshots are byte for byte as before.

## Goals

- One pure function, `decide` in `src/sharing/policy.ts`, that answers `send`, `withdraw` or `keep` with a reason code and the policy epoch (a number that changes whenever the policy changes, defined under Storage), for one destination, one item (an entry or a memory) and the item's ledger records; the team server and the sync client call it and nothing else decides (D-11).
- A level per project and destination (`local`, `knowledge`, `all`; shown as Nothing, Knowledge, Knowledge and memories), stored in a `sharing` schema namespace of `module_migrations` so `user_version` stays 7 (D-8, D-9); the level bound to the project's `remote:` alias (D-16).
- Two settings in `<data dir>/env`, the roots that may leave and the allowed areas with root-qualified ids, where the environment can only narrow and loading fails closed (D-12, D-13, D-14).
- The fixed meaning of `all` for memories (D-15) and the fixed lists of what accompanies an item and what never leaves (D-19).
- Every change recorded with its source; the epoch moved by every change; a widening confirmed before it is saved; a restore that resets the levels (D-16, D-17).
- The Collaboration card turned into the Sharing page, the level in Across projects, `wizardingcode-mem share`, a `status` line, a `doctor` check, `GET /api/sharing` (D-18, D-20).
- `docs/adr/0025-sharing-policy.md`; the pinned hook and MCP snapshots unchanged.

## Non-Goals

- The team server, the sync client, any network, any device token (parts 5 and 6). This part writes no `sharing_ledger` row.
- Redaction's implementation at the edge: part 6 passes every text through `redactForSend`; this part names it and pins it (D-19).
- Sharing the author's user rules (D-4); a per-entry override; a second destination (D-9); a `uid` column on `memories`.
- Deleting anything: a lowered level recalls nothing; part 6's `withdraw` does.
- Any change on the hook path: no hook reads the policy; `openDb` creates the tables on the first open as it does for every module.

## Open Questions

None open. One narrowing the person may want to relax later: D-16 lets only a project with a `remote:` alias be set above Nothing, because the Collaboration card and part 5 identify a project across machines by its remote; a local-only repository cannot be shared until part 5 has another identity for it, and the ADR says so.

## Critical User Journeys

1. **Nothing leaves by default.** Given a store with projects, memories and knowledge entries and no sharing choice made, when the person runs `wizardingcode-mem status`, then the line is `sharing   nothing leaves this machine (every default)`; when `decide` is asked about any entry or memory for the destination `team`, then it answers `keep` with the reason `level-local` or `root-off`.
2. **A project's knowledge is allowed to leave.** Given the project `ai-mem` with a `remote:` alias, when the person opens it in Across projects and chooses Knowledge in the dock, then a dialog says how many entries and memories would leave once a team server is configured (counted under the proposed level), and nothing is sent now; when they confirm, then the row shows Knowledge, `sharing_changes` has a row from the viewer, the epoch changed, and `wizardingcode-mem share status` run in that folder prints `this      ai-mem: Knowledge (bound to remote:github.com/…)`.
3. **The workspace's sales knowledge is allowed.** Given the workspace taxonomy with `sales` and `finance` and six active sales entries, when the person turns on Workspace knowledge and the `sales` toggle in Settings → Sharing, then the toggle says `6 entries`, and `decide` answers `send` for an active workspace sales entry, `keep` `status` for a draft, `keep` `area-private` for a finance entry, `keep` `problem` for an entry with a problem; when a project at Knowledge holds a sales entry, then it is `send` too, because the workspace's areas govern project repositories.
4. **Memories at Knowledge and memories.** Given a project set to Knowledge and memories, when `decide` is asked about its memories, then an active memory is `send`, a stale one `keep` `stale`, a branch-scoped one `keep` `branch-scope`, an imported one `keep` `imported`, a user rule `keep` `user-rule`, an archived one `keep` `status`; the Sharing page and the dock say that memories have no area.
5. **The environment cannot widen.** Given the file with roots `workspace` and the environment with `workspace,shared`, when the policy loads, then the roots are `workspace` only, `doctor` warns that the environment's roots value was ignored, and the Sharing page shows no lock; given the environment with `none`, then the roots are empty, the page shows the roots locked as set by the environment, and `status` says so.
6. **Lowering and the ledger.** Given a `sharing_ledger` row saying `workspace/pricing-rules` version 3 was sent to `team`, when the person removes `sales` from the areas, then `decide` answers `withdraw` with the reason `area-private` for that entry; when the row carries `withdrawn_at`, then `keep`; when the entry's content changed and it is still allowed, then `send`.
7. **A restore resets the levels.** Given a backup taken while two projects were above Nothing, when the person restores it, then every level is Nothing, one `sharing_changes` row has source `restore`, the epoch differs from every earlier one, the CLI prints `backup: sharing levels reset to Nothing (2 projects); set them again`, and the roots and areas in the env file are as they were.
8. **Nothing else changes.** Given no sharing choice made, when the hooks run and the MCP server lists its tools, then the pinned snapshots are byte for byte the same; when a 0.6.0 binary opens the store, then `user_version` is 7 and the `sharing_*` tables are ignored.

## Design

### Levels, roots, areas, destinations

`src/sharing/types.ts` (a leaf: no imports from the store or the modules):

- `type Level = "local" | "knowledge" | "all"`; `LEVELS` in that order; `LEVEL_LABELS = { local: "Nothing", knowledge: "Knowledge", all: "Knowledge and memories" }`; `widens(from, to)` is true when `to` is later in `LEVELS`.
- `type Root = "workspace" | "shared"`; `ROOTS = ["workspace", "shared"]`.
- `SHARE_AREA = /^(workspace|shared):([a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,39})$/`: a root-qualified area id; its second group is part 1's area id pattern (`^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]*$`, at most 40 characters), and a test runs twelve strings through both.
- `TEAM_DESTINATION = "team"`: the one destination of this part. Part 5 binds it to the configured server; a second server is a second destination and starts with every level `local`.
- `NONE = "none"`: the token that means the empty list in either setting.
- `REASONS`, the fixed list of reason codes, and `type Reason = (typeof REASONS)[number]`: `allowed`, `already-sent`, `policy-failed`, `unknown-destination`, `received-same`, `received-elsewhere`, `problem`, `status`, `root-off`, `level-local`, `level-knowledge`, `disabled`, `unbound-alias`, `no-project`, `area-private`, `area-inert`, `stale`, `branch-scope`, `imported`, `user-rule`; `REASON_TEXT: Record<Reason, string>`, one sentence each (listed in the plan; used by `share explain` and the viewer).
- `entryUid(scope, remoteAlias, id)`: `workspace:<uuid>`, `shared:<uuid>` or `project:<remote alias>:<uuid>`, so an entry file copied between repositories has two uids; `memoryUid(generation, id)`: `<generation>:<row id>`.
- `remoteAliasOf(cwd)` is added in this part to `src/core/project.ts`: the `remote:` alias `identify` computes through `normalizeRemote`, or null, so it matches `project_aliases` exactly.
- `src/sharing/levels.ts`, a second leaf over `types.ts` that both `store.ts` and `policy.ts` import, so neither imports the other: `levelOf(policy, projectId, destination)` (defined under The policy), `levelKey`, `epochOf(counter, settings)` (the derived epoch, defined under Storage).
- `src/sharing/patch.ts`, a pure module the viewer imports as it imports `types.ts`: `buildSharingPatch({ roots, areas, taxonomies, inert, sources })` turns the page's toggles into the settings patch: the roots and the areas as sorted comma lists or `none`, an area id dropped when it is in no taxonomy (D-14), and a key left out of the patch when its source is `env`; a unit test proves each rule.

### Settings

`SETTING_KEYS` gains `WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_SHARE_ROOTS` and `WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_SHARE_AREAS`. Both are comma lists: roots of `workspace` and `shared`; areas of root-qualified ids. `none` is the empty list; an absent key is the empty list. `RULES`: roots, "a comma list of workspace and shared, or none"; areas, "a comma list of root-qualified area ids such as workspace:sales, or none"; the message never repeats the value. `validatePatch` normalises both as `moduleList` does (trim, lower-case) and de-duplicates.

The two keys live in the env file because the mission and the brief put the sharing settings there and in the viewer's Settings, keys named like environment variables (the brief's Request and Constraints); the levels live in the database because the project list is there (D-1). The cost of two stores is a baseline and an audit step, stated under Storage.

**Narrow-only (D-13).** `loadSettings` parses the env file once, as today; for these two keys it reads the parsed map and the process environment apart, two lookups each (an empty environment variable counts as unset, as `rawSettings` already treats it). The effective list is the file's list when the environment is unset, and the intersection of the file's and the environment's lists when the environment is set; `none` in the environment is the empty list. One rule for `publicSettings`: the key's `source` is `env` when the environment is set and removes at least one id of the file's list (the file's list is not a subset of the environment's), `file` when the file holds the key and the environment is unset or removes nothing, `default` otherwise; its `value` is the effective list as comma text, or `none` when empty. So a partial narrowing locks the control with "Set by the environment."; an environment that only adds ids changes nothing, shows `file`, and `doctor` says how many ids the environment added ("the environment's <key> names <n> more than the file; ignored").

**Fail closed (D-14).** Any token that does not match (`Sales!`, `sales` without a root, `workspace:`, a path separator) empties that key's whole effective list and records a problem naming the token's position, never its value ("<key>: token 3 is not a root-qualified area id; the list was emptied"). An invalid token in the environment's value empties the environment's list, which removes every id of the file's: with a file value the source is `env`, the control is locked, and the problem names the token; with no file value the source is `default`. `Settings` gains `sharing: { roots: Root[]; areas: string[]; problems: string[] }`, every list sorted, the problems per key in a fixed order (the file token, the environment token, the ignored count).

### Storage: the `sharing` namespace (D-8, D-9, D-11, D-17)

`src/sharing/migrations.ts`: `SHARING_MODULE = "sharing"`, `SHARING_SCHEMA_VERSION = 1`, `SHARING_MIGRATIONS` (one, version 1, `sharing_policy`: the SQL of `src/sharing/migrations/0001_sharing_policy.sql` and a `run` step that mints `sharing.generation` when absent). `src/engine/module-schemas.ts` lists it after knowledge's: `MODULE_SCHEMAS = [knowledge, sharing]`. The namespace is not an `EngineModule`: it has no contribution and no tools; the schema list is the one place that knows it. No table has a foreign key or a trigger on a core table; 0.6.0 opens the store (`user_version` 7) and ignores them. Part 1's runner is read as deciding a backup per schema, by that schema's own recorded version, so the first `sharing` migration takes none even where knowledge's row exists; the plan has the case that proves it, and the change to make if part 1's runner reads otherwise.

```sql
CREATE TABLE sharing_levels (
  project_id INTEGER NOT NULL,
  destination TEXT NOT NULL,
  level TEXT NOT NULL,              -- checked in code against LEVELS
  bound_alias TEXT NOT NULL,        -- the remote: alias the level was set under
  set_at INTEGER NOT NULL,
  source TEXT NOT NULL,             -- viewer | cli
  PRIMARY KEY (project_id, destination)
) WITHOUT ROWID;

CREATE TABLE sharing_ledger (
  destination TEXT NOT NULL,
  item_kind TEXT NOT NULL,          -- entry | memory
  item_uid TEXT NOT NULL,           -- entryUid(...) for an entry; <generation>:<row id> for a local memory; remote:<server uid> for a received memory
  direction TEXT NOT NULL,          -- sent | received
  version INTEGER NOT NULL,
  hash TEXT NOT NULL,
  policy_epoch INTEGER NOT NULL,
  at INTEGER NOT NULL,
  withdrawn_at INTEGER,
  PRIMARY KEY (destination, item_kind, item_uid, direction)
) WITHOUT ROWID;

CREATE TABLE sharing_changes (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  at INTEGER NOT NULL,
  source TEXT NOT NULL,             -- viewer | cli | restore | file | system
  what TEXT NOT NULL,               -- project:<id> | roots | areas | levels
  old TEXT NOT NULL,
  new TEXT NOT NULL
);
CREATE INDEX sharing_changes_at ON sharing_changes(at);
```

The `meta` keys and the rules around them, one per bullet:

- `sharing.counter`: an integer as text, 0 when absent; every change made through the viewer, the CLI, a restore or the system reconcile adds one, inside the same `withWrite` as the change.
- **The epoch** is derived, never stored: `epoch = counter * 2^20 + hash20(settings)`, where `hash20` is a 20-bit FNV-1a over the canonical text `roots|areas` of the lists in force: the roots in force, and the areas in force after the taxonomy filter (an id that is listed and not in its root's taxonomy is not in force). A hand edit of the env file therefore changes the epoch with no write, and so does an area added to a taxonomy when it brings a listed id into force; the counter keeps two policies with the same lists apart over time; `decide` returns it, the ledger records it, and part 6 re-reads the policy at every batch and stops the run when the epoch differs from the one it started with. Equality means the same lists and no change since.
- `sharing.settings_seen`: the baseline, JSON `{ roots: [...], areas: [...] }` of the file's own lists (never narrowed by any environment, so two processes with different environments agree) as last recorded by the viewer, the CLI or a restore; a missing baseline reads as the empty lists. It exists only for the audit: a difference between the file and the baseline is a hand edit nobody recorded.
- `sharing.generation`: eight random hex characters, minted by the migration's `run` step when absent and renewed by every restore (never by `moveStore`): the store's generation, which scopes a memory's identity on this machine. Read-only everywhere else (`generationOf` answers null when absent and never writes).
- A memory's identity: memories are never deleted (no `DELETE FROM memories` in `src/`), so a row id is stable on this machine until a restore, which brings back an older copy whose later ids are given again to other memories; the generation makes the two lives distinct, and after a restore every memory has a new uid (the server's items of the old generation are part 6's reconciliation to withdraw). A store file copied to another machine keeps its generation, so part 5's device token scopes every uid on the server (device, then generation, then row id) and two machines never collide; the ADR names the copied-store case.
- An entry's identity: `entryUid` scopes the UUID by its repository, so one file in two repositories is two items.
- Received items: a memory received from a destination is stored as a new local row with `origin = 'imported'` (which `decide` never sends) and `judge = 'team:<destination>'` as the marker that tells it from a claude-mem import; its `item_uid` is `remote:<the server's uid>`; a received entry keeps its scoped uid. Part 6 writes a received item and its `received` ledger record in one `withWrite` (the index row and the ledger row together; the file write precedes them), so no received item exists without its record. Two existing paths act on `origin = 'imported'` today, `history replace-imports` (`src/backfill/replace.ts`) and `rejudge` (`src/judge/rejudge.ts`); part 6, when received memories first exist, must narrow both to rows whose `judge` is not `team:…`, and the ADR records that requirement with the marker decided here.
- The ledger after a restore: `sharing_ledger` rows are not touched by a restore; the copy's ledger is the copy's, and part 6 reconciles it with the server.

`src/sharing/store.ts`:

- `levelRows(db)`: every row (an unknown `level` string is reported as a problem by the loader and treated as `local`). A row stored at `local`, which only a hand can write, reads as no row: `local` with the reason `level-local`, whatever the project's state, and no problem.
- The loader's problem sentences, beside the two quoted further down: an id listed and not in its root's taxonomy, `areas not in the taxonomy, ignored: <ids>`; a level on a project with memory off, `memory is off for <name>; its level is Nothing until you turn memory on and set it again`; a level string this binary does not know, `<name> has a level this version does not know; its level is Nothing until you set it again`; a load that threw, `the sharing policy could not be loaded (<message>); nothing leaves`. `share status` prints the inert ids on its `areas` line and does not repeat that problem.
- `previewLevel(policy, projectId, level)`: `{ current: Level; widened: boolean }` against the effective level (`levelOf`), the pre-write check every path uses before it asks or writes.
- `setLevel(db, { projectId, destination, level, source, now, confirm, remoteAlias? })`, in steps, inside one `withWrite`, reading every input under the write lock (the caller's policy is only what it showed the person; a concurrent process may have moved the row): (1) `level` must be in `LEVELS` (`invalid`), and the destination must be one this binary configures (`DESTINATIONS`; any other is a programming error and throws, since a row stored for an unconfigured destination would take force unasked the day a later part adds it); (2) the project row must exist (`no-project`); (3) above `local`, the project must not be disabled (`disabled`) and must have a `remote:` alias in `project_aliases` (`no-remote`). These structural refusals come before the confirmation: a person is never asked to confirm a widening that would then be refused, and the viewer's route and the CLI get them from this one function; (4) the stored row, the project's `disabled` flag and its aliases are read inside the transaction and the effective level computed from them as the loader would; a widening against it without `confirm` is refused (`needs-confirm`); (5) `local`: a stored row is deleted, also one that only reads `local` (a disabled project, a lost alias), so an explicit Nothing does not come back when memory is turned on again or the alias returns; the change is logged, with the stored level as `old` when the row only read `local`; `boundAlias` is answered empty and the outcome's `old` is the effective level; (6) above `local`, the alias the level is bound to is the `remoteAlias` given (the CLI passes the current directory's) when it belongs to the project; else the alias the stored row is already bound to, while the project still has it, so a change from the viewer, which passes no alias, never moves the binding and with it every project entry's uid; else the first of the project's remote aliases ordered by `alias`; (7) the row is written with `bound_alias`, `set_at`, `source`, a change row `project:<id>` with the effective `old` and the new level, the counter added one; (8) the same effective level twice writes nothing and logs nothing, except a stored row whose alias is gone: writing it above `local` is a widening from `local` (it needs `confirm`), rebinds it to the current alias, is logged as `what: project:<id>`, `old: rebind`, `new: <level>`, and answers `rebound: true`. The sentences for `no-project`, `no-remote`, `disabled`, `invalid` and `needs-confirm` come from `setLevelMessage`, not from `REASON_TEXT`.
- `previewSettingsPatch(dataDir, changes)`: `{ key, old, new, widened }[]` over the file's own lists (what the person chose, before any environment), the pre-write check the CLI and the route use.
- `applySettingsPatch(db, dataDir, changes, source, now, confirm, io)`: the one path that writes the env file when the patch holds a sharing key. Inside one `withWrite`: a hand edit nobody recorded (the file's lists differ from the baseline) is recorded first, with the code `reconcileSettings` runs (one `file` row per list that differs, the counter added one), so that a save never swallows it and no caller has to reconcile first; then the old file lists are read, a widening without `confirm` is refused (`needs-confirm`, which rolls the whole transaction back, the `file` row included: the edit stays pending and the loader keeps reporting it), one change row per sharing key whose list the patch changes (`roots` or `areas`, old and new as comma text or `none`), the counter added one when at least one list changed, the whole patch written to the file through `io.writeEnvFile` (atomic, as `saveSettings` does; the file last, so a database failure leaves it untouched), and `sharing.settings_seen` set to the file's new lists. A sharing key the patch repeats with the list the file already holds writes no row and does not move the counter: the viewer's Save sends both keys every time, and a save that changes nothing must not grow the log or change the epoch. (Found while building Phase B; the first text said "one change row per sharing key present".) A file write that throws rolls the transaction back: no row, no counter change, and the file is as it was. A crash between the file write and the commit leaves the file written and no row, which the next `reconcileSettings` records with source `file`; the ADR says so. A patch with no sharing key goes through `saveSettings` as today. The CLI's `share roots` and `share areas` and the viewer's `PUT /api/settings` both call this function; there is no second path.
- `reconcileSettings(db, dataDir, now)`: inside one `withWrite` it reads the env file's own lists and `sharing.settings_seen` again (never the caller's policy), and for each list that differs it writes a change row with source `file`, adds one to the counter once, and sets the baseline; nothing when equal. An audit, never needed for correctness: the epoch already moved with the file.
- `reconcileLevels(db, now)`: inside one `withWrite` it reads every level row with its project's `disabled` flag and aliases again, and for every row whose downgrade still holds (`disabled`, or `bound_alias` not among the project's aliases) it deletes the row and logs `project:<id>` with the stored level as `old`, `local` as `new` and source `system`, adding one to the counter once; a row the person has just set again is read as it now is and kept. So re-enabling a project or a returning alias never brings a level back without a new choice. The window between the downgrade and the next look is a stated limit: no production path disables a project or removes a `remote:` alias today (`resolveProject` only adds them), and a later part that adds one must call `reconcileLevels` in the same transaction, which the ADR records.
- Both reconciles are called by `GET /api/sharing`, by every `share` subcommand before it acts (load, reconcile, load again), and by the sync at every batch (part 6's rule, named in the contract); never by `status` or `doctor`, which open the store read-only and report what is pending ("the sharing settings changed outside the viewer and the CLI; open the Sharing page or run share status to record it"; "<project> lost the git remote its level was bound to; its level is Nothing until you set it again"). A hand edit that widens cannot be confirmed after the fact: the file is the person's, and a process with write access to it is past the boundary, as an agent with a shell is (objection O-1, advisory, in `work.json` and `conclave.md`).
- `resetLevels(db, now, epochFloorCounter, fileLists)`: in one `withWrite`, deletes every `sharing_levels` row when the table exists (by name in `sqlite_master`, any type), logs one `levels` change when rows were deleted (`old`: `<n> projects above Nothing`, `new`: `every project Nothing`, source `restore`), sets `sharing.counter` to `max(epochFloorCounter, the copy's counter) + 1` so the counter never falls back, renews `sharing.generation`, and sets `sharing.settings_seen` to `fileLists` (the current env file's lists) so no false `file` change follows a restore; returns the number of rows deleted. It runs on any copy, with or without the sharing tables (the `meta` table is core).
- `lastChange(db)`, `recentChanges(db, limit)`, `counterOf(db)`, `generationOf(db)`.

### The policy and `decide` (D-11, D-14, D-15, D-16)

`src/sharing/policy.ts`:

```ts
interface SharingPolicy {
  epoch: number;                         // counter * 2^20 + hash20(settings)
  closed: boolean;                       // a load failure: nothing leaves
  destinations: ReadonlySet<string>;     // { "team" } in this part; the roots and areas apply to these only
  projects: ReadonlySet<number>;         // the known project ids (every row of `projects`)
  roots: ReadonlySet<Root>;
  areas: ReadonlySet<string>;            // root-qualified, in their root's taxonomy
  inert: readonly string[];              // listed but in no taxonomy
  settings: { roots: Root[]; areas: string[] };   // the effective lists before the taxonomy filter (areas ∪ inert)
  fileLists: { roots: Root[]; areas: string[] };  // the file's own lists, for the audit baseline
  settingsSeen: { roots: Root[]; areas: string[] };  // the baseline, empty lists when missing
  levels: ReadonlyMap<string, LevelRow>; // keyed levelKey(projectId, destination) = `${projectId}@${destination}`
  problems: readonly string[];           // what was ignored, emptied, pending or failed
}
interface LevelRow { projectId: number; destination: string; level: Level; boundAlias: string; reason: Reason }
// `reason` is `allowed` for a level in force, or the downgrade (`disabled`, `unbound-alias`) that made it read `local`.

type Item = EntryItem | MemoryItem;      // the predicate's input
interface EntryItem { kind: "entry"; uid: string; scope: "workspace" | "shared" | "project"; projectId: number;
  area: string; status: string; problem: string | null; version: number; hash: string }
interface MemoryItem { kind: "memory"; uid: string; projectId: number; status: string; stale: boolean;
  scope: "project" | "branch"; origin: string; isUserRule: boolean; version: number; hash: string | null }
// a memory's `hash` is null when the caller did not compute it; `decide` treats null against a live sent record as changed
interface LedgerRecord { destination: string; direction: "sent" | "received"; version: number;
  hash: string; withdrawnAt: number | null }
interface Decision { answer: "send" | "withdraw" | "keep"; reason: Reason; epoch: number }
```

`loadPolicy(db, dataDir, env): SharingPolicy` never throws. In steps:

1. `loadSettings`: the effective roots and areas, the file's own lists, the problems.
2. Whether `sharing_levels` exists (one query of `sqlite_master` by name; part 1's `moduleSchemaState` answers `current` when `module_migrations` is missing, so it cannot tell a store that never ran the migration): a missing table gives no levels and no problem (`status` and `doctor` on a store nobody migrated).
3. `moduleSchemaState(db, "sharing", 1)`: `too-new` gives a closed policy with the problem "the sharing schema is newer than this binary".
4. The known project ids.
5. The levels joined to `projects` (`disabled`) and `project_aliases` (does `bound_alias` still belong to the project): a row whose project is gone is dropped; a disabled project's level reads `local` (reason `disabled`, a problem naming the project); a row whose alias the project lost reads `local` (reason `unbound-alias`, a problem naming the project); an unknown level string reads `local` (a problem).
6. The taxonomies: `knowledge/workspace/taxonomy.md` and `knowledge/shared/taxonomy.md` under the data directory read with part 1's `parseTaxonomy` (one file each, no git, ids and labels; a missing or unparseable file means every id of that root is inert).
7. `sharing.counter`, `sharing.settings_seen`; the epoch computed; when the file's lists differ from the baseline (empty lists when missing), a problem "the sharing settings changed outside the viewer and the CLI; open the Sharing page or run share status to record it".
8. Any thrown error gives `{ closed: true, problems: [message], epoch: 0, everything else empty }` and is logged once per process under `sharing` (a module-level flag; `resetSharingLog()` clears it for tests).

`levelOf(policy, projectId, destination): { level: Level; reason: Reason }`: `unknown-destination` when the destination is not in `policy.destinations`; `no-project` when `projectId` is 0 or not in `policy.projects` (an orphan folder or an ambiguous manifest has no project); `level-local` for a known project without a row for that destination (a project at Nothing has no row: `setLevel` to `local` deletes it); else the row's level with `allowed` or its downgrade reason. The roots and the areas have no destination in their key: they apply to the destinations in `policy.destinations`, which this part fixes to `team`; a second destination needs its own keys, and until a later part adds them `decide` answers `keep`, `unknown-destination` for it whatever the roots and areas say (the ADR records this as the rule parts 5 and 6 build against).

`decide(policy, destination, item, records): Decision`, pure, in this order:

1. `policy.closed` → `keep`, `policy-failed`.
2. The destination not in `policy.destinations` → `keep`, `unknown-destination`.
3. A record with `direction: "received"` for this item: from another destination → `keep`, `received-elsewhere`; from this destination with the same `hash` as the record → `keep`, `received-same` (the unchanged received version is never echoed back); from this destination with another hash (this machine wrote a later version) → on to step 4, so a person's improvement of received knowledge may go back to its origin as an update. A received memory is also stored with `origin = 'imported'`, which step 4 keeps, so a received memory never leaves even when its record was lost; a received entry's guard is its record, written with the entry in one transaction.
4. Allowed or not, with the reason:
   - an entry: `problem !== null` → `problem`; `status !== "active"` → `status`; scope `workspace` or `shared`: the root must be in `policy.roots`, else `root-off`; scope `project`: `levelOf` must give `knowledge` or `all`, else its reason (`level-local`, `disabled`, `unbound-alias`, `no-project`); the area: `<root>:<area>` with the root `workspace` for workspace and project scopes and `shared` for the shared scope, in `policy.areas` → allowed; in `policy.inert` → `area-inert`; else `area-private`.
   - a memory: `levelOf` must give `all`, else `level-local` (a `knowledge` level reads `level-knowledge`) or its downgrade reason; `status !== "active"` → `status`; `stale` → `stale`; `scope === "branch"` → `branch-scope`; `origin === "imported"` → `imported`; `isUserRule` → `user-rule`.
5. With `sent` the record of this destination with `direction: "sent"`: allowed and `sent` not withdrawn with the same `hash` (a null memory hash counts as changed) → `keep`, `already-sent`; allowed otherwise → `send`, `allowed`; not allowed and `sent` not withdrawn → `withdraw`, the reason; not allowed otherwise → `keep`, the reason. `version` is informational; the hash decides equality.

The meaning of `all` is step 4's memory list and nothing else (D-15): a level that sends another set is a new name in `LEVELS`, and `LEVEL_LABELS` grows with it.

`redactForSend(text, env): Redacted` (in `src/sharing/edge.ts`): the one function the sync passes every outgoing text through; it calls `redact` from `src/core/redact.ts` with the store's `env`. It exists in this part so the contract has a name and a pinned test (`<private>x</private>` becomes `[PRIVATE]`; a seeded key is replaced); it is the only text-level protection: an entry body is sent as written apart from what `redact` recognises, so a person must not write a secret into one, and the ADR says so.

### What leaves with an item, and what never does (D-10, D-19)

The contract parts 5 and 6 build against, recorded in the ADR and pinned by the contract test:

- **The outgoing payload types** (`src/sharing/types.ts`): `OutgoingEntry = { kind: "entry"; uid; scope; slug; remoteAlias: string | null; frontmatter: string; body: string; version; hash }` and `OutgoingMemory = { kind: "memory"; uid; remoteAlias: string; title; body; memoryKind; importance; status; createdAt; updatedAt; version; hash }`: an allowed entry leaves as its current frontmatter and body as the file holds them, its scope, its slug, and its project's `remote:` alias (the bound alias; null for the workspace and the shared repository); an allowed memory as its redacted title and body, kind, importance, status, created and updated times, the project's `remote:` alias. An entry's `version` and `hash` are part 1's (the file's version and the hash of its bytes). A memory's `version` is `updated_at`, informational; its `hash` is the first 16 hex of the SHA-256 over the payload fields `memoryKind`, `importance`, `status`, redacted `title`, redacted `body`, joined by newlines in that order, computed after redaction, so a rejudge changes it and a use count does not. The sync reads an entry's file after part 1's `ensureFresh` and a clean `git status` for that path, and passes `decide` the parsed values, never an index row; a dirty or problem file never leaves (D-10).
- **Never**: `turns`, `sessions`, the history index, `hook_runs`, `injections`, `memory_files`, `terms`, `branch`, `commit_sha`, `transcript_path`, `path:` and `gitdir:` aliases, the project `key`, commit e-mails, git history, proposals, drafts, archived entries, decided proposals, `user_memories`. The list is the ADR's prose; the guard in code is the payload types' key sets.
- **Redaction at the edge**: every text that leaves passes `redactForSend` in the process that sends it; the Sharing page's promise rests on it.
- **The contract test** (`tests/unit/sharing-contract.test.ts`, AC-19) pins the key sets of the predicate's input (`EntryItem`, `MemoryItem`) and of the payload (`OutgoingEntry`, `OutgoingMemory`), the memory hash's field order, `REASONS` against `REASON_TEXT`, `redactForSend`, `levelKey`, `entryUid` and `memoryUid`. What it guards: a field added to any of the four types fails the test until the lists and the ADR change together. What it does not guard: a payload built outside those types; the ADR says part 6 must build its payloads from them.

### Counts, lists and previews (D-20)

`src/sharing/report.ts`: `wouldLeave(db, dataDir, env, policy, options: { projectId?: number; list?: boolean; fresh?: boolean; now: number; refresh?: (repo: Repo) => void }): Report`. Entries come from `knowledge_entries` joined to `knowledge_repos` (0 when the tables do not exist); memories from `memories` LEFT JOIN `user_memories` (the join is the input `isUserRule`, not a rule), pre-filtered in SQL by the project ids whose effective level is `all` only (the `memories_rank` index; D-15's rules live in `decide` alone, and a test asserts the counts equal `decide` over every memory of those projects), selecting `id, project_id, status, stale, scope, origin, updated_at` and never `title` or `body` for counting; each candidate goes through `decide` with its ledger records. The ledger is read by `ledgerRecords(db, destination, kind)`: one scan of the rows of that kind that belong to that destination or were received (`item_kind = ? AND (destination = ? OR direction = 'received')`, since `decide` answers `received-elsewhere` from a record of another destination), grouped by uid in memory, never an `IN (…)` list, since SQLite binds at most 32,766 parameters and a store has 90,000 memories. A memory's `hash` is computed only for the candidates that have a ledger record, by `withHashes` in chunks of 500 ids; every other candidate carries `hash: null`. A project entry's uid takes the alias its level is bound to, also when the project has since lost that alias, so that what was sent under it can still be matched and withdrawn; with no stored level, the project's first `remote:` alias by name, which is the one `setLevel` would bind; with no remote at all, the empty alias, for a project that can never be set above `local`. The memory candidates are read only when some project is at `all`, which implies the migration ran, so the generation is read with `getMeta` and nothing is written.

`Report = { counts: { entries, memories }; perRoot: Record<Root, { entries: number; areaTotals: Record<string, number> }>; perProject: { id, level, entries, memories }[]; items?: { entries: { scope, projectId, slug, title, area }[]; memories: { id, projectId, title }[]; more: { entries, memories } } }`. `counts`, `perRoot.<root>.entries` and `perProject` are what leaves under the policy given; `areaTotals` are the active, problem-free entries per area id in the repositories the root governs (the workspace and every project repository for `workspace`; the shared repository for `shared`), whatever the area's own state and the root switch, so a toggle shows what it would release. Items are capped at 500 each with `more` counts. With `fresh: true` (the viewer's `GET /api/sharing` and the CLI; never `status`, never the preview) it first runs `refresh` (default part 1's `ensureFresh`) on the workspace repository, then the shared, then the project repositories of projects above `local` by project id ascending, the only repositories that can release entries; a `refresh` that throws is caught per repository, logged under `sharing.refresh` with the repository's path, and the counts use the index as it is. Nothing here runs on a hook.

**Previews.** `withChange(policy, change: PolicyChange, dataDir): SharingPolicy` builds, in memory, the policy with a change applied: `{ level?: { projectId, level }, roots?, areas? }`, any of the three together (the areas filtered by the taxonomies as the loader does; the epoch recomputed over the lists in force with the counter one higher, so a preview never carries the stored policy's epoch; a closed policy is returned as it is; the environment does not narrow a preview, and a level preview does not check the project, which the route does first). The dialogs ask `POST /api/sharing/preview` with that change and show `wouldLeave` under the result with `fresh: false` (no git on a dialog; the index as the page's last `GET` refreshed it); nothing is written.

The figures used in this document: about ninety projects and 90,000 memories are the person's own store as ADR 0018 records it; 10,000 entries is a chosen ceiling; the loader's 20 ms is a target the gate measures and no test pins; the memory count has a 1,000 ms target and a 5,000 ms test bound (a loaded runner must not fail the suite; a regression by an order of magnitude still does).

### Changes, confirmation, epoch (D-17)

A widening is: a level later in `LEVELS` than the effective one (`previewLevel`); a root or an area added to the file's list (`previewSettingsPatch`). Every path checks before it writes with those two functions, and `setLevel` and `applySettingsPatch` refuse a widening without `confirm` all the same. The viewer confirms a widening with a dialog (`Confirm.vue`, tone `warn`, action "Share"; title "Share more of <project name>?" in the dock and "Share more knowledge?" on the Sharing page) whose description shows the preview's counts ("12 knowledge entries and 0 memories of ai-mem would leave for your team's server once one is configured. The project question is `Share <label> of <name> with the team? [y/N] `; the roots and areas question is `Share more knowledge with the team? (<the new list>) [y/N] `. Lowering asks nothing. Every change through the viewer, the CLI or a reconcile writes `sharing_changes` and adds one to the counter; a hand edit of the env file moves the epoch at once and is recorded with source `file` the next time the viewer, the CLI or the sync looks, and `doctor` says when one is pending. Nothing lowers what is already on the server: the Sharing page says so, and `withdraw` is part 6's.

### Identity (D-16)

`setLevel` refuses a project without a `remote:` alias (`no-remote`) and a disabled project (`disabled`). The loader downgrades a level whose `bound_alias` the project no longer carries or whose project is disabled; `reconcileLevels` removes such a row and logs it with source `system`; until then `doctor` and `status` report it as pending. A project repository whose `knowledge_repos.project_id` is 0, an orphan folder (no row) and an ambiguous manifest (part 1's `listRepos`) have no project and answer `no-project`. `restoreBackup` resets the levels of the copy before the swap. `moveStore` carries the tables untouched. A folder reused by a repository without a remote keeps the old project id (`resolveProject`'s rule, ADR 0018) and so the old level; the ADR names it, and disabling the project or setting it to Nothing is the person's recourse.

### The command (D-20)

`wizardingcode-mem share <sub>` (`src/cli/commands/share.ts`), opening the store with `openDb` on `resolvePaths().storeDir` and a 5,000 ms busy timeout, and loading the policy, running both reconciles and loading again before every subcommand acts:

- `project <level> [--yes]`: `<level>` one of `local`, `knowledge`, `all` (exit 64 otherwise, with the usage naming the labels); the project of the current directory through `findProject` (never creates; "this folder is not a known project; start a session here first", exit 1); the remote alias of the current directory (`remoteAliasOf`) is passed to `setLevel`, with `confirm` set only by `--yes`; `setLevel` refuses `no-remote` and `disabled` before it would ask for a confirmation, so each prints its sentence and exits 1 with no question; on `needs-confirm` the command asks on a TTY (`Share <the label in lower case> of <name> with the team?`) and calls again with `confirm` true, or, with no terminal, exits 1 with `widening needs --yes when nobody can confirm`; a typed no exits 1 with `not confirmed; nothing was changed`; it prints `sharing: <name> → <label>`, followed by ` (unchanged)` when the level was already that.
- `roots <list|none> [--yes]` and `areas <list|none> [--yes]`: validate through `validatePatch`, `previewSettingsPatch` first, ask on a widening, save through `applySettingsPatch` with source `cli` and `confirm` true; print `sharing: roots → <list or none>` / `sharing: areas → <list or none>`; an invalid list prints the rule's message and exits 64.
- `status [--list]`: the lines
  ```
  sharing   epoch <n> · last change: <what> <old> → <new>, <when>, from the <source>   (or: epoch <n> · no change yet)
  roots     <list or none>
  areas     <list or none>[ (<inert ids> not in the taxonomy; ignored)]
  projects  <k> knowledge · <a> knowledge and memories · <l> nothing
  this      <name>: <label>[ (bound to <alias>)] | not a known project
  leaves    <e> entries · <m> memories
  ```
  then, with `--list`, `entry   <scope>/<slug>  <area>  <title>` and `memory  <id>  <title>` lines, at most 500 each and `+<n> more`; with `fresh: true`; `problems` printed as `problem   <text>` lines after `areas`.
- `explain <scope>/<slug> | memory <id>`: finds the item (the current project's repository for `project/<slug>`; `memory <id>` by row id), reads its ledger records, prints `<ref>: <answer> · <reason> · <REASON_TEXT>`, or `not found` with exit 1.
- Exit codes as part 1's commands: 0, 1 with `wizardingcode-mem: <message>` on stderr, 64 with the usage. The arguments are parsed before the store is opened, so a usage error opens no store and runs no reconcile. When the policy loads closed (a newer sharing schema, a load that threw), the reconciles are skipped and `project`, `roots` and `areas` are refused with the policy's problem; `status` and `explain` still answer. `<when>` in the status line is the ISO time; `leaves` reads `1 entry · 0 memories` in the singular.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A column on `projects` by core migration 8.** Rejected: `user_version` 8 makes every 0.6.0 binary refuse the store on every hook, which part 1 built the module namespace to avoid, and a `CHECK` on the enum can only be widened by rebuilding a table five others reference (D-8).
- **Roots and areas as rows in the `sharing` namespace, beside the levels.** Weighed and rejected for the reason under Settings: the mission and the brief put these settings in the env file and the viewer's Settings.
- **A stored epoch bumped by every change.** Rejected: a hand edit of the env file could not move it without a write-on-read in every reader; the derived epoch moves with the file and the counter keeps policies apart over time.
- **A level per project alone.** Rejected: one choice would reach every destination a later part adds, and a predicate without a destination argument cannot be fixed once data has left (D-9).
- **Syncing repositories by `git push`.** Rejected for parts 5 and 6: history, decided proposals and past private-area content would carry private text past the predicate (D-10).
- **A boolean predicate.** Rejected: the sync needs `withdraw` to tell "never shared" from "shared, now withdrawn", and a person asks why (D-11).
- **One flat area list.** Rejected: part 1 gives the workspace and the shared repository separate taxonomies, so one id would switch two meanings (D-12).
- **The shared repository always allowed to leave.** Rejected: nothing leaves until named; it has a switch like the workspace (D-12).
- **The environment wins (the settings rule).** Rejected for the sharing keys: one stray variable would widen from one process only, and the viewer's switch could not turn it off (D-13).
- **A lenient parse of a hand-edited list.** Rejected: a typo must mean "not shared", never a wider list (D-14).
- **A `uid` column on `memories`.** Rejected: a core migration for an id that is stable on this machine; the store's generation and part 5's device token scope it (D-11).
- **Dropping ids from the area list inside part 1's `removeArea`.** Rejected: a cross-part coupling; inert at load and dropped at the viewer's save covers it (D-14).
- **The levels in the env file.** Rejected: the project list is in the database and the viewer edits rows (D-1).
- **A doctor warning for "sharing configured but no team".** Rejected: a permanent warning badge with no fix (D-20).
- **Counting over every repository with `ensureFresh`.** Rejected: ninety project repositories could spawn ninety git calls; only the repositories that can release entries are refreshed.
- **Leaving the predicate to part 5.** Rejected: the brief's acceptance is that parts 5 and 6 call one predicate and nothing else decides; its contract is fixed here, with tests, before any data leaves.
- **A second terminal prompt beside `install.ts`'s.** Rejected: the existing prompt is extracted into `src/cli/prompt.ts` and both commands use it.

## Risks and rollback

- **A restore silently lowers sharing.** Intended (D-16): a copy taken under a wider policy must not re-grant it; the CLI and the viewer say what was reset, and the person sets the levels again. The roots and the areas, in the env file, are not in the backup and keep their current values; the ADR says so.
- **A restore gives memory ids a second life.** `memories.id` is a plain integer key: an older copy hands the ids of memories sent after it to new memories. The store's generation, renewed by the restore, makes their uids new, so the server never takes a new memory for an old one; the old generation's items on the server are part 6's reconciliation to withdraw, and the copy's `sharing_ledger` is left as it is. A store file copied to another machine keeps its generation; part 5's device token keeps the two machines apart. Both named in the ADR as the limits of a row id as identity.
- **A hand edit of the env file widens without a prompt.** The epoch moves at once; the edit is recorded by `reconcileSettings` with source `file` the next time the viewer, the CLI or the sync looks, and `doctor` warns while it is pending. Nothing can confirm it after the fact: a process with write access to the file is past the boundary, as with the shell (objection O-1).
- **A downgraded level comes back.** A disabled project or a lost alias reads Nothing at once; the row is removed by `reconcileLevels` the next time the viewer, the CLI or the sync looks, so re-enabling or a returning alias never restores the level silently; `doctor` warns while the row is pending. The window before that look is a limit: nothing in the product disables a project or removes a `remote:` alias today, and a later part that adds such a path must reconcile in the same transaction (the ADR records it).
- **A received memory meets the import paths.** `history replace-imports` and `rejudge` act on `origin = 'imported'`; a received memory carries that origin and the marker `judge = 'team:<destination>'`, and part 6 must narrow both paths before any memory is received; the ADR records it.
- **A received entry's improvements.** The unchanged received version is never echoed back; a version this machine wrote since may go back as an update, so a member's improvement reaches the team; a received memory never leaves again (it is `imported`). Said in the ADR.
- **Counts can differ from what a sync later sends.** `status` reads the index as it is and says so; the viewer and the CLI refresh the repositories that can release entries first; the sync reads the bytes it sends (D-10).
- **An agent with a shell can widen sharing.** `share project all --yes` runs; the boundary is the local user account; every change is logged with its source and `share status` shows the last one.
- **A reused folder keeps a project's level.** `resolveProject` keeps the id for a repository without a remote at a known path (ADR 0018); the ADR names it; the person's recourse is Nothing or disabling.
- **A 0.6.0 viewer saves settings.** `renderEnvFile` keeps unknown keys, so the two sharing keys survive; a later re-upgrade reads them again while the levels, if a v7 copy was restored, are Nothing; stated in the ADR.
- **Rollback.** Parts 1 to 4 ship in one release; the rollback target is 0.6.0, which opens the store (`user_version` 7) and ignores the `sharing_*` tables and `module_migrations`' new row; the two settings keys are kept by `renderEnvFile` and ignored.

## Non-Functional Requirements

- **Hook path.** No hook reads the policy. `loadSettings` on a hook reads two more keys from the file map it already parses and from the process environment. The first `openDb` after the upgrade runs the `sharing` migration once under the write lock (three small tables and one `meta` row, no backup, since the backup is decided per schema). `tests/perf/budgets.test.ts` keeps its budgets.
- **The loader.** Four queries (`sqlite_master`, `sharing_levels` joined to `projects` and `project_aliases`, `module_migrations`, `meta`) and two small file reads; a target of 20 ms on the gate machine with the person's ninety projects, measured by the gate and pinned by no test. `wouldLeave` with `fresh: false` over 90,000 memories and 10,000 entries reads no title or body for counting (one indexed query over the `all` projects, one over the knowledge index, one over the ledger's records); the target is 1,000 ms, the test's bound 5,000 ms, the gate's report records the figure; with `fresh: true` the git calls of the refreshed repositories dominate, bounded by the person's choices.
- **Security and privacy.** Fail closed on any load failure; the environment never widens; a widening asks and is refused without confirmation in the store itself; every change is logged; the counts never move data; nothing in this part opens the network. The contract test fails when a field is added to the four pinned types; a payload built outside them is not covered, and the ADR says part 6 must build from them.
- **Rollout.** The first open creates the tables and mints the generation; every default is local, so an upgraded store behaves as before until the person chooses.
