## Findings

**What this proposes**
Before any team server exists, the person chooses what may leave the machine later. There are three controls. Each project gets a level: `local` (nothing), `knowledge` (its knowledge repository) or `all` (its knowledge and its active memories, but not the person's own rules). One on/off switch covers the workspace knowledge repository. One global list names the knowledge areas that may be shared. An entry leaves only if two things hold: its repository is allowed out (the shared repository always is, the workspace one when the switch is on, a project's when its level is `knowledge` or `all`), and its area is on the list. A pure predicate in `src/sharing/policy.ts` answers this, and parts 5 and 6 (the team server and the sync) will call it. The project level is a new column on `projects`. The switch and the area list are two keys in `<data dir>/env`. The viewer has a Sharing card in Settings and a level selector on each row of Across projects. The CLI gets `share project <level>` and `share status`. `status` and `doctor` print counts of what would leave.

**How the existing system works, per this document**
Settings are a fixed list of keys, each with a validation rule, stored in `<data dir>/env` at 0600. The environment wins over the file, and the viewer reads and writes the file through `/api/settings`. Projects are rows in `projects`, and a `disabled` flag means "memory off": such a project feeds no other project. Part 1 keeps knowledge in git repositories per scope (workspace, one per project, shared). Each entry has an area from a taxonomy, and agents' changes wait as proposals. Today nothing leaves the machine except TypeSafe judgements, and only when a key is set.

- F-1 · Major · The policy, the levels and the area list together · Levels and areas combine as AND, and the default area list is empty. So a person who sets a project to `knowledge` or `all` and changes nothing else sees no project knowledge leave, and the shared repository's knowledge does not leave either. The selector looks like it acted and nothing changed. The concept never says the viewer row or `share project` warns "0 entries would leave: no area is shared". A person will believe they shared something they did not.
- F-2 · Major · The policy, `mayShareMemory` · Memories have no area, so at `all` every active memory of the project leaves whatever the area list says. A person who kept `finance` private will assume finance talk in that project's memories stays home. The selector and the card need to say that areas protect knowledge only. As written, nothing tells the person this, and they would miss a real leak.
- F-3 · Major · The policy, "the shared scope always (it is the team's)", against the Assumptions "subject to the area list too" · The sketch says the shared repository is always shared, then makes it depend on the area list, and the defaults say everything is local. A person cannot tell from the card whether their shared repository is going out today. The card has no control for the shared repository, so the only way to keep it home is to empty the area list, which also stops every project and the workspace.
- F-4 · Major · Storage, `WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_SHARE_AREAS` · One list covers both taxonomies, the workspace one and the shared one. The same area id, `sales` for example, can carry different labels in each, and there is no way to share `sales` from the shared repository while keeping the workspace's `sales` private. The card "lists from the workspace and the shared taxonomies" but does not say whether a repeated id is one toggle or two, or which label it shows. Someone building the card would guess, and a person would toggle something wider than they meant.
- F-5 · Major · The viewer, the area toggles · An area is a global switch: turning `sales` on releases it at once from the shared repository, from the workspace when the switch is on, and from every project at `knowledge` or `all`. The card shows a toggle per area with no count of what that toggle releases. The person cannot see how far one click reaches before they make it.
- F-6 · Major · How to know what would leave (`GET /api/sharing`, `share status`, the `status` line) · Only counts are offered. A person deciding what leaves needs the list itself: which entries, which memories, which projects. Without a list (for example `share status --list`, or a "would leave" filter in the Knowledge and Memories views), "12 entries would leave" cannot be checked against the person's intent. This is exactly the question the person asked: "how do I know".
- F-7 · Major · What "leaves" means before a team exists · The card's line "Nothing leaves this machine until a team is configured (part 5)" puts an internal part number into product text. It also does not say that once a team is set up, everything already allowed goes out on the first sync with no further prompt, or that something sent cannot be taken back by later making it private. A person treats today's choices as a rehearsal, not as a standing authorisation.
- F-8 · Major · The scope of "what leaves", against README Privacy and part 2's `Judge.relate` · Part 2 sends redacted knowledge titles and bodies, private areas included, to TypeSafe when a key is set. The sharing policy does not govern that. A person who reads the card's "this is what will" and `status`'s "what the policy lets out" will conclude that private knowledge never leaves. The concept never says the policy covers the team server only.
- F-9 · Major · The policy, "a proposal never leaves", drafts and archived entries · The predicate decides per entry, but the knowledge lives in git repositories, and part 1's Risks mention "the team server's copy once part 6 syncs the shared scope". The concept does not say whether anything other than the entry text can leave: the history, decided proposals, the commits of a private-area entry. A reader cannot tell whether "private" holds once a sync works on whole repositories.
- F-10 · Major · "A disabled project is `local` whatever its column says" · The column keeps its value while memory is off. Turning memory back on revives the old level, possibly `all`, silently. A person who switched memory off to stop a sensitive project, then switched it on again, will not expect its memories to be cleared to leave. The concept does not say whether re-enabling resets the level to `local` or warns.
- F-11 · Major · The viewer and the CLI, the environment overriding the file · The settings rule is that the environment wins. Backups.vue shows that case ("Set by the environment.", controls disabled), but the Sharing card's behaviour is not stated. If `WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_SHARE_AREAS` is set in a shell profile, the toggles must show as locked, or the person flips them, sees "saved", and nothing changes.
- F-12 · Major · Storage, the level in the database and the rest in the env file · `wizardingcode-mem backup` copies the database and not the env file, so restoring an older backup silently brings back old project levels, possibly wider, while the area list stays as it is now. The person is not told that a restore changes what leaves.
- F-13 · Nit · CLI, `share project <level>` · Only the project of the current directory can be set from the CLI, and there are no `share workspace on|off` or `share area add|remove <id>` commands. A terminal-only person must edit `env` by hand for two of the three controls. The concept also does not say what `share project` prints outside a known project, or for a disabled one: an error, a no-op, or the stored value.
- F-14 · Nit · The workspace switch · "The workspace repository" is defined only in part 1, and here it is a bare word. A person meets "workspace" elsewhere too: the README's link signal "one workspace", and editor workspaces. The card label needs to say "the knowledge in `<data dir>/knowledge/workspace/`, your cross-project entries", and say that it does not cover project repositories, which are rooted on the workspace taxonomy.
- F-15 · Nit · The three level names · `knowledge` is already the name of the module, of an entry kind and of a migration. `all` overstates what it covers: user rules, proposals, drafts and archived memories are excluded, and knowledge is still filtered by area. Labels such as "Nothing / Knowledge / Knowledge and memories", each with a one-line help that names what is excluded, would match what a person expects from a selector.
- F-16 · Nit · Doctor, "warn … said as information" · "Sharing is configured but no team is" is a normal state until part 5 ships. A `warn` for it gives everyone who set sharing a permanent warning that has no fix. The concept says "warn" and "information" in the same breath without picking one.
- F-17 · Nit · The viewer, two places · Project levels live in Across projects, while the switch and the areas live in Settings → Sharing. A person who opens the Sharing card sees no project levels and no link to them. The card should at least show "N projects share knowledge, M share memories" and point to where they are set, or the person will think the card is the whole policy.
- F-18 · Nit · Storage, an unknown area "kept and warned by `doctor`" · A new area added later to a taxonomy starts private. That is the safe default, but it is not said to the person, who may assume that sharing "everything" today includes areas added tomorrow. The card could say "new areas start private".
- F-19 · Nit · The viewer, changing a level · Widening a level (`local` to `all`) is a privacy decision, but no confirmation is mentioned, unlike the Restore modal in Backups.vue. A dialog that states what would leave (the counts from `GET /api/sharing`) would match how the product already confirms consequential actions.
