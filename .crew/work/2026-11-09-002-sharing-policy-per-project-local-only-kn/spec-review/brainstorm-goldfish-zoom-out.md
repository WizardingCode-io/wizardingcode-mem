## Findings
- F-1 · Major · Storage, "Core migration 8" · The concept puts the level in core migration 8. That raises `user_version` to 8, and `src/store/db.ts:201` makes 0.6.0 throw `SchemaTooNewError` on any newer database (ADR 0018 notes that every agent's binary has to move together). Parts 1 and 2 avoided exactly this: they keep `user_version` at 7 and use module migrations so that "the rollback target is 0.6.0, which ignores the tables". A builder following this concept would remove that rollback for the whole journey's release without noticing. The simpler choice is the module namespace part 1 built (`MODULE_SCHEMAS`): a `sharing_projects(project_id, level)` table with no foreign key, where a missing row means `local`. That also needs no `CHECK` rebuild later if a level is added.
- F-2 · Major · The policy, `mayShareEntry` per entry · The predicate works per entry and filters on status and area. But part 1 keeps each scope as a git repository, and the obvious way to sync one (for example the shared scope) is a push, which carries every commit: drafts, decided proposals and entries in private areas, including entries that were private in the past and later moved. The concept never says whether part 6 sends entries or repositories, so the interface is fixed before the one fact that decides whether a per-entry answer can be enforced at all. Either state that sync sends exported entries and never git history, or wait to fix the predicate's shape until part 5 or 6 names the unit it sends.
- F-3 · Major · The policy, area list over the shared scope; Assumptions · `WIZARDINGCODE_MEM_SHARE_AREAS` is one flat list of area ids. Part 1's D-9 gives the workspace and the shared repository separate taxonomies, so `sales` in the workspace and `sales` in the shared repository are different areas that one id now switches together. On top of that, the list starts empty and applies to the shared scope too, so the team's own repository sends nothing until someone ticks every area. A reader would build a shared scope that is silently empty by default. Simpler: the shared scope is the team's and is not filtered by area (or its areas start shared), and the list is either keyed by root (`workspace:sales`) or applies only to the workspace and project repositories.
- F-4 · Major · Design sketch, the viewer · The product already makes a promise that this design ignores. `ui/src/components/settings/Team.vue` ("Collaborative mode", marked soon) tells the user "Sharing is opt-in, per project. Anything marked private, or inside a `<private>` block, never leaves your machine." A search of `src/` finds no `<private>` handling, and the concept neither covers that promise in the predicate nor removes it. It also adds a new Sharing card beside Team.vue instead of turning that card into the switch, as the card itself says will happen ("this page becomes the switch"). Use the existing card, and either honour the `<private>` promise in the predicate or reword the card.
- F-5 · Major · Hypothesis, the case against · Nothing reads the policy yet. Parts 5 and 6 are not designed, nothing leaves the machine today, and "every default is local" is already true because no sync exists. Building the predicate, two settings, a new route, `GET /api/sharing`, a CLI subcommand, a status line and three doctor warnings now freezes an interface whose inputs (`scope, projectId, area, status, isUserRule`) are guessed for callers that do not exist. The user's Q-3 needs the choice made before any data leaves, not before the sync exists. The smallest answer is the stored choices plus the viewer controls in this part, with the predicate, its counts and its doctor lines landing in the same part as their first reader.
- F-6 · Nit · CLI and status · There are three read-outs of one policy that nothing consumes: the `status` line, `share status` and `GET /api/sharing` with counts of what "would leave". `share status` repeats `status`. Keeping the `status` line and the viewer is enough until sync exists. Counting the memories that would leave is a full scan whose figure means nothing yet.
- F-7 · Nit · Storage, where the policy lives · The policy is split across two stores with different precedence. The two workspace keys follow `loadSettings`, where the environment wins over the file, so a shell or `.envrc` variable can turn `SHARE_WORKSPACE` on without the person touching the file. The per-project level has no environment override at all. That is acceptable, but the ADR should say it for a privacy control (`publicSettings` does show the source in the viewer).
- F-8 · Nit · The policy, workspace switch · The workspace switch (`on`/`off`) is a second vocabulary next to the three levels. Treating the workspace as one more row with a level (`local` or `knowledge`) would remove a setting key, a rule and a separate viewer control.
- F-9 · Nit · The policy, `isUserRule` and disabled projects · Reuse is mostly right: "disabled means local" builds on ADR 0018's flag, and `isUserMemory` (`src/store/links.ts:232`) already answers `isUserRule`, so the concept should name it rather than leave room for a new query. Separately, a search of `src/` finds no code that sets `projects.disabled` to 1. A doctor warning for "set to knowledge or all but disabled" therefore covers a state the product's own tools cannot easily reach. That is worth confirming before writing the warning.
- F-10 · Nit · Design sketch altitude · SQL DDL, environment key names and the exact help text belong in the spec, not in a brainstorm concept. Here they make the column-on-`projects` choice (F-1) look already settled.
